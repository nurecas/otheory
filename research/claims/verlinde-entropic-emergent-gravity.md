steelman: "Building on Jacobson's (1995) derivation of the Einstein equations from thermodynamic first principles (entropy, temperature, and the Bekenstein-Hawking area law) and holographic ideas, Verlinde proposed (2010) that Newtonian gravity, and later (2017) an extended framework, could arise as an entropic force from an underlying microscopic theory rather than being fundamental, deriving Newton's law and (in the relativistic extension) suggesting galaxy rotation curves could be explained by an additional 'dark gravity' contribution from entanglement entropy of de Sitter space — without positing dark matter particles. Verlinde and collaborators found reasonable qualitative fits to some galaxy rotation-curve data."
strongest_objection: "The Bullet Cluster observation — where gravitational lensing (implying mass) is spatially offset from the visible baryonic matter after a cluster collision — is a serious problem for any purely baryon-based emergent-gravity account, since it strongly suggests a genuine (dark) mass component that moves independently of normal matter, which is difficult to reproduce in Verlinde's framework. Additionally, Dai & Stojkovic (2017) raised technical inconsistencies in the derivation; a later rebuttal by Yoon (2020, arXiv:2003.03198) contests their reading, showing the technical dispute is still live and unresolved rather than settled in either direction. No consensus derivation of General Relativity's full dynamical content (including cosmological structure formation and the Bullet Cluster) from entropic gravity currently exists, and it remains a minority position within the gravity/dark-matter research community, most of which favors particle dark matter (WIMPs, axions) or other modified-gravity approaches (MOND-family)."
falsifiability: "Falsifiable in principle: entropic/emergent gravity predicts specific relations between galactic baryonic mass distribution and rotation-curve dynamics (similar in spirit to MOND) that can be tested against large galaxy surveys, and it predicts no genuinely independent dark-matter component, which the Bullet Cluster and cluster-lensing data already challenge. Currently the balance of evidence (especially cluster-scale lensing and CMB structure-formation data, both of which favor particle dark matter with an extra non-baryonic component) disfavors the strong 'dark matter is not needed' version of the proposal, though weaker/qualified versions remain debated. This test is already feasible and has been run; the result to date is unfavorable to the strong version of the claim."

Verlinde's entropic/emergent gravity is E3: a heterodox but credentialed, published proposal (JHEP, SciPost) taking seriously the idea that gravity and spacetime are thermodynamic/informational in origin, extending Bekenstein-Hawking-style reasoning (see black-hole-entropy-area-law) to claim gravity itself is not fundamental — but it faces a serious empirical challenge from the Bullet Cluster and lacks the broad consensus support enjoyed by particle dark-matter models. It is the physics claim closest in spirit to O Theory's "gravity/spacetime as emergent from information" framing, and it is important to state plainly: even its author does not extend it to any claim about consciousness, and its empirical standing is contested, not settled.
