steelman: "Wigner (1961) imagined a friend measuring a system inside a sealed lab, then Wigner himself treating the friend-plus-system as one large superposed quantum system from outside — raising whether the friend experienced one definite outcome all along, or only got one upon Wigner's later measurement. Frauchiger and Renner (2018) formalized an extended version with four agents reasoning about each other's measurements using quantum theory itself, and proved as a theorem that three assumptions — (Q) quantum theory is universally valid for any system including agents, (C) different agents' conclusions, when shared, are logically consistent, and (S) a measurement has one single outcome — cannot all be simultaneously true. This is a genuine no-go theorem, not merely a puzzle, sharpening the measurement problem into a statement about the limits of applying quantum theory to agents who themselves use quantum theory."
strongest_objection: "Which assumption to give up is exactly the interpretation-dependent question the paper leaves open, and the literature is split: Everettians deny (S), Bohmians and objective-collapse theorists deny (Q)'s universal validity in the relevant sense, and QBists/relational-QM proponents argue their frameworks reject an implicit fourth assumption (that all agents' statements can be combined into one absolute narrative) and so escape the paradox altogether — meaning the theorem's force depends on contested premises about what 'the same theory used by different agents' even means, and some (e.g., Lazarovici & Hubert) have disputed the argument's validity outright, not just its interpretation."
falsifiability: "The theorem is a logical/mathematical result given the stated premises (proven, not an empirical claim); no laboratory version of the full four-agent gedankenexperiment (which requires literally superposing a conscious or measuring agent) has been performed, though small-scale photonic analogues testing formally related structures have been reported. It does not admit a direct empirical test of 'which assumption is false.'"
