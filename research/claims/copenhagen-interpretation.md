steelman: "There is no single canonical 'Copenhagen interpretation' — Bohr, Heisenberg, and later expositors differed — but the common core is: the quantum formalism gives probabilities for outcomes of measurements described in classical/everyday language, wavefunction 'collapse' is a rule for updating predictions rather than a physical process needing further explanation, and the theory is complete for all predictive purposes. This deflationary stance avoids committing to unobservable mechanisms and matches how most working physicists use QM without confusion."
strongest_objection: "It relies on an unexplained and seemingly arbitrary 'Heisenberg cut' between a quantum system and a classical measuring apparatus, with no principled physical account of where the cut lies (this is essentially a restatement of the measurement problem, not a solution to it). Historically, 'Copenhagen' is also a retrospectively-constructed label (Howard 2004) papering over real disagreements between Bohr and Heisenberg, so citing it as one settled doctrine is itself historically shaky."
falsifiability: "As an interpretation it makes the same predictions as the bare formalism and is not experimentally distinguished from rival interpretations; there is no possible experiment that would confirm or refute 'Copenhagen' as opposed to, say, decoherence-plus-no-further-commitment. This is why it sits at E2, not E1, despite being the historically dominant textbook stance."

RED-TEAM NOTES (2026-07-07): All three sources verified. Faye's SEP entry is the standard reference and correctly
summarized (no single canonical doctrine; epistemic/instrumentalist common core). Bohr (1928), Nature 121, 580-590,
is the correct citation for the complementarity paper (Como lecture). Howard (2004), Philosophy of Science 71(5),
669-682, is a real, well-known paper arguing "Copenhagen interpretation" as a unified doctrine is a later
historiographic construction — accurately cited and its thesis correctly represented.

Tier confirmed E2 (mainstream-speculative/interpretive, not experimentally distinguished from rivals) — correctly
NOT E1, since this is a family of interpretive stances riding on top of the E1 formalism, not itself an
experimentally confirmed claim. The falsifiability field is honest: no experiment distinguishes Copenhagen from
other no-new-physics interpretations, exactly matching CLAUDE.md's expectation that most interpretations state
"no experiment distinguishes them."

Important and correct point of discipline in the closing note: standard Copenhagen does NOT require a *conscious*
observer — a classical/macroscopic measuring apparatus suffices (this matches Bohr's actual writings, which speak
of classical apparatus and language, not minds). This correctly and explicitly blocks the common conflation with
von-neumann-wigner-consciousness-collapse, which is essential to keeping the three senses of "observer" (physics
measurement/decoherence E1, technical/interpretive E2, literal consciousness-causes-collapse E3) distinct as
required. No changes needed beyond confirming status.
