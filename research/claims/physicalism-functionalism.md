steelman: "Physicalism is the default of naturalistic metaphysics: every successful reduction in the history of science (life, heat, magnetism) has gone the physicalist way, and mental states covary lawfully with brain states with no known exception. Functionalism strengthens this by defining mental states via their causal/functional role, explaining multiple realizability (why an octopus or a machine could in principle share a mental state type with a human) while remaining compatible with a physical substrate for every actual realizer."
strongest_objection: "The knowledge argument, the conceivability of zombies, and the explanatory gap all target physicalism at exactly the point of phenomenal (as opposed to functional/behavioral) consciousness: functionalism in particular is charged with defining away the very thing needing explanation, since two systems could be functionally identical while one lacks any 'what it is like' (Block's 'absent qualia' worry, illustrated by his 'China brain' thought experiment, discussed at length in SEP's functionalism entry sec. 5.5.1). Whether this objection succeeds is itself contested and not resolvable by further data."
falsifiability: "Not empirically decidable even in principle as a metaphysical thesis about what mental states ultimately are; particular psychophysical correlations are empirical, but the identity/constitution claim goes beyond any possible correlation data. Physicalism/functionalism is the majority position among analytic philosophers of mind and the default backdrop against which O Theory's "consciousness is fundamental" claim is a direct rival, not a refinement — the two frameworks disagree about which is ontologically basic (matter/function or experience). SEP's physicalism entry itself notes we live in "an overwhelmingly physicalist or materialist intellectual culture," and that skeptics of physicalism bear a correspondingly higher argumentative burden — confirming this is the field's majority default, not a claim on a level playing field with its rivals. This is a philosophical position (E4), not an empirical finding: no experiment adjudicates the metaphysical constitution question even though the underlying neuroscience is uncontroversial. It must be represented as the strongest orthodox alternative to O Theory's core commitment, not dismissed.
