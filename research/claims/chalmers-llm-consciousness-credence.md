steelman: "Chalmers gives one of the most careful treatments available: he grants LLMs pass some behavioral tests suggestive of understanding and self-modeling, but methodically lists the standard scientific theories' requirements (recurrent processing theories, global workspace theory, higher-order theories, embodiment/world-modeling, unified agency) that current transformer-based LLMs largely fail to satisfy architecturally, concluding it is 'somewhat unlikely' current LLMs are conscious while explicitly refusing to rule it out, and flagging that near-future systems addressing these architectural gaps (recurrence, persistent memory, embodiment) could change the assessment substantially within a decade. He assigns a specific ballpark figure — on the order of 25% or more credence for conscious 'LLM+' systems with these features within roughly a decade — rather than leaving 'nontrivial' unquantified."
strongest_objection: "This is a philosopher's structured argument, not an experiment: it depends on accepting that the listed theories (GWT, HOT, recurrent processing) are the right criteria for consciousness, which is itself contested (see butlin-long-indicator-properties-framework, gnwt-global-workspace), and on treating architectural features as reliable proxies for phenomenal experience, which no test can currently confirm. Illusionists and strict biological-naturalists (who hold consciousness requires specific biological substrates) would reject the whole framing that gives current or future LLMs any nonzero credence at all."
falsifiability: "Not decidable in principle by any current test — this is why it is philosophical (E4), not empirical. Chalmers frames it as a credence under uncertainty, revisable as architectures change, rather than a claim that could be confirmed or refuted by a specific experiment."
