steelman: "Katz's 'hard constructivism' argues: (A) a mystic's conceptual scheme completely determines the nature and content of their mystical experience, and (B) mystics across traditions hold pervasively different conceptual schemes; therefore (C) there is no single cross-cultural mystical experience, only many differently-constructed ones. This forces scholars to take seriously how much doctrine, language, and expectation shape reports before they are ever compared, and is a genuine corrective to naive perennialism that flattens traditions into one thing. Forman and other essentialists counter with the Pure Consciousness Event (content-free awareness) as a case where premise A plausibly fails, and note that mystics' own surprise/novelty reports and initial 'thin descriptions' are hard to square with total conceptual determination."
strongest_objection: "Neither side has a decisive empirical test: this is an argument about the relationship between concept and experience, not a measurable dispute, so it is correctly filed E4 rather than E2/E3. Critics of Katz note his premise A is stated so strongly it risks being true by definitional fiat (if 'experience' just means 'concept-laden content,' the conclusion is smuggled in); critics of Forman note the PCE itself cannot be shown, even in principle, to be free of tacit conceptual shaping, since the only access to it is retrospective report already couched in a tradition's language. No consensus position exists in the current philosophy-of-religion literature (per SEP); most contemporary scholars occupy positions between the two poles (soft constructivism) rather than endorsing either extreme, which this claim should not obscure by presenting it as a clean two-sided fight."
falsifiability: "Not empirically decidable even in principle; a conceptual/interpretive dispute about the relationship between language, culture, and experience. This is the central E4 SCHOLARLY-EPISTEMOLOGY dispute of the domain, not a report and not an empirical finding: it is an argument about how to interpret the reports catalogued elsewhere (Stace's typology, Forman's PCE, jhāna/samādhi). It must not be conflated with the empirical psychometric question of whether questionnaire items cluster the same way across cultures (a testable, weaker claim — see meq30/Hood claims) nor with the metaphysical question of what a unitive experience, if genuinely common, would tell us about reality (see mystical-experience-epistemic-status). O Theory's "one O-Source behind many traditions' reports" reading is explicitly a perennialist bet on one side of this unresolved dispute, not a settled fact.

Distinct from, but cross-referenced to, perennial-philosophy-common-core-thesis (comparative-religion domain): that claim is the metaphysical Traditionalist thesis (Huxley/Schuon) that traditions converge on the same ultimate reality; this claim is the narrower scholarly dispute over whether the experiences themselves (independent of any metaphysical payoff) are culture-independent in structure. The two are adjacent and share the Katz/SEP sourcing but should not be merged — one is about the ontology traditions point to, the other is about whether reports are conceptually constructed.
