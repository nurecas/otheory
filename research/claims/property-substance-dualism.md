steelman: "Property dualists argue that even granting a single physical substance, the qualitative, 'what it is like' character of experience is not merely a repackaging of physical/functional facts but a genuinely emergent, irreducible property — a position motivated by the same explanatory-gap intuitions behind the knowledge argument and the conceivability of zombies. Substance dualism (Descartes) further argues the subject of experience is not identical to any material thing, motivated by the conceivability of disembodied existence and unity-of-consciousness considerations."
strongest_objection: "Substance dualism faces the interaction problem: if mind is a nonphysical substance, how does it exert causal influence on the physical brain without violating the causal closure of physics, a principle well supported by physics? SEP reports this as 'a widely accepted opinion' that substance dualism is 'no longer a respectable option' while property dualism 'must be taken seriously' (citing Weir 2023) — though Robinson notes this consensus is itself regularly challenged, and substance dualism retains defenders among philosophers and some neuroscientists. Property dualism, meanwhile, inherits its own version of the interaction/causal-exclusion problem (Kim's exclusion argument) if mental properties are to do any causal work distinct from their physical bases."
falsifiability: "Not empirically decidable even in principle: no experiment can show a phenomenal property is or is not 'identical to' versus 'distinct from but correlated with' a physical property, since the correlational data is equally compatible with both readings. Argued by reason; E4."

Property dualism is the closest orthodox-analytic cousin to O Theory's dual-aspect framing, but the two differ sharply: standard property dualism treats matter as ontologically primary with mental properties as an additional (emergent or brute) layer, whereas O Theory's dual-aspect monism (and idealist variants) treat neither as more fundamental than the other, or treat experience as prior. Substance dualism is included for completeness and contrast but is now a minority view even among dualists, chiefly due to the interaction problem — though "no longer respectable" is SEP's report of a widely-held professional opinion, not an uncontested fact; Robinson notes it is itself challenged by several philosophers and some neuroscientists. Both dualist variants are argued positions (E4), not empirical results.
