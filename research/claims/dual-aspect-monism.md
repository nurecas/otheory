steelman: "Traceable to Spinoza's single substance (Deus sive Natura) with mind and extension as two of its infinite attributes, dual-aspect monism avoids substance dualism's interaction problem (there is only one substance) and avoids physicalism's and idealism's asymmetry (neither aspect is derivative of, or reducible to, the other). It is attractive precisely because psychophysical correlation is a brute, symmetric fact on this view rather than a puzzle to be explained away in one direction. Its clearest contemporary articulation, the Pauli-Jung conjecture (documented in SEP sec. 5), treats mind and matter as complementary manifestations of one underlying, holistic order, with correlations arising from a shared 'common ground' rather than direct causal interaction — explicitly designed to respect the causal closure of physics."
strongest_objection: "Critics press exactly the trilemma raised against neutral monism (mentalism/materialism/experience-problem suspicions, per SEP's neutral-monism entry sec. 4) plus a further one specific to the 'two aspects' move: unless something more precise is said about what unifies the two aspects and why there are exactly two (rather than one, or many), 'dual-aspect' risks being a redescription of the correlation rather than an explanation of it — the same complaint leveled at any brute psychophysical identity claim. It also inherits its own combination-style problem in explaining why the aspects are bound to just the boundaries of a single subject. Note also that SEP frames the Pauli-Jung conjecture's supporting 'mind-matter correlation' material as evidence for meaningful (synchronistic) correlations in clinical psychology, not as any physical test of the dual-aspect metaphysics itself — the metaphysical claim (that one underlying reality has two co-fundamental aspects) remains untestable regardless of that correlational literature's own evidentiary status."
falsifiability: "Not empirically decidable even in principle: any psychophysical correlation data is equally compatible with property dualism, neutral monism, or dual-aspect monism, since the dispute concerns the metaphysical relation between the two sides of the correlation, not the correlation itself. Argued by reason; E4."
