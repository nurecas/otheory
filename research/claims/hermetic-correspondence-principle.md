steelman: "As a hermeneutic and contemplative principle, correspondence between 'above' (cosmos/divine) and 'below' (individual/material) organizes a coherent, historically deep symbolic system used in astrology, alchemy, and Renaissance Hermetic philosophy (Ficino, Pico) to interpret nature as meaningful rather than inert. It functioned as a genuine interpretive lens for pre-modern natural philosophy, not a physical mechanism."
strongest_objection: "Correspondence is an interpretive/symbolic principle, not a physical law or testable causal claim: it asserts pattern-resemblance between scales as meaningful without a specified physical mechanism, and is not falsifiable. Even textually, the earliest Arabic version is subtly weaker than the popular paraphrase — it describes a relationship of derivation ('from'), not the looser correspondence/similarity ('like to') that later Latin and English versions imply, so the modern proverb has drifted from its own source. When modern popularizations (see the Kybalion claim) present it as if it were an ancient, systematized 'science,' that overstates both its antiquity and its epistemic status. Symbol/practice, not fact."
falsifiability: "Symbolic/practice, not an empirical claim. No experiment could confirm or refute a general 'as above, so below' correspondence; treating it as physics would be a category error, so it is correctly excluded from the falsifiability requirement that applies to physics claims."

The correspondence principle is E5: a real, textually attested symbolic framework from the Hermetic tradition (Emerald Tablet, Corpus Hermeticum), honestly represented as that tradition's interpretive lens on nature, not as an empirical finding. It is the direct precedent for O Theory's own "as above, so below" imagery linking cosmic and personal/instance scales — which should be labeled a metaphor/analogy bridge, not a shared-mathematics claim. See `kybalion-modern-new-thought` for the crucial historical correction that the popular systematized "Seven Hermetic Principles" version of this idea is a 1908 invention, not ancient doctrine.
