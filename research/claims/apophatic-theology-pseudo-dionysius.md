In the Mystical Theology, Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite teaches that God utterly transcends every name, concept, and even the negation of concepts, so that the highest approach to God is an unknowing ("agnosia") beyond both affirmation and denial — a method that became foundational for later Christian apophatic mysticism (Eckhart, the Cloud of Unknowing) and influenced Jewish and Islamic negative theology. Represented here as this tradition's own theological-contemplative claim (E5); it is a disciplined argument and practice about the limits of language applied to the divine, not an empirical claim, and any resemblance to modern "ineffability" language in mysticism studies is a structural analogy across traditions, not identity.
