steelman: "Von Neumann (1932) showed mathematically that the 'cut' between quantum system and classical measuring apparatus can be pushed arbitrarily far along the chain of physical interaction — apparatus, retina, optic nerve, brain — without changing any prediction, because quantum mechanics never forces you to stop describing things unitarily at any particular point. Wigner (1961) argued that only at the point of a conscious observer's subjective awareness does it become natural to say collapse has 'really' happened, since only there is there something (an experience) for the superposition to become determinate FOR. Chalmers & McQueen (2022) give the most rigorous recent defense, showing it can be made mathematically precise (combining a formal theory of consciousness, integrated information theory, with an explicit collapse dynamics, continuous spontaneous localization) and is not logically inconsistent, connecting it to integrated-information-style measures of when a physical system counts as an observer."
strongest_objection: "It is a minority position among physicists and philosophers of physics, not the mainstream reading even of Copenhagen (Bohr's own view required only a classical, not conscious, apparatus — see copenhagen-interpretation). Decoherence theory independently explains why superpositions become practically unobservable at the scale of any measuring device, brain included, without invoking consciousness at all, removing the original motivation for pushing the cut that far (see qm-decoherence). De Barros & Oas (2017) go further than merely noting practical difficulty: they argue that under reasonable assumptions about consciousness, the hypothesis is not just hard to test but structurally unfalsifiable in its most general form — proposed tests (e.g., interference experiments on a system a conscious observer looks at vs. does not) fail because keeping a conscious brain in the required isolated quantum superposition is thermodynamically/practically impossible, and the paper's own conclusion is that the hypothesis, as generally stated, resists falsification. Chalmers & McQueen's own 2022 treatment partially cuts the other way: they note that the SIMPLEST versions of a consciousness-collapse theory (naive quantum Zeno-style collapse-on-observation) are already empirically falsified by the observed quantum Zeno effect itself, while only more baroque, parameter-laden versions remain compatible with current evidence — the same ad-hoc-parameter pattern that costs GRW/CSL credibility, but without GRW/CSL's mindless, universal, in-principle-testable dynamics."
falsifiability: "Mixed and worth stating precisely rather than in one direction: as originally stated by von Neumann/Wigner it is framed as in-principle falsifiable (predicting different physical behavior depending on whether a conscious or unconscious system does the 'observing'), and the simplest concrete versions ARE already falsified (Chalmers & McQueen 2022 note the quantum Zeno effect refutes naive versions). But de Barros & Oas (2017) show that the surviving, more careful versions of the hypothesis evade essentially every proposed experimental test for structural (not merely practical) reasons, making the hypothesis, in the forms that remain live, very close to unfalsifiable. No experiment has ever detected a consciousness-dependent physical effect on a quantum outcome. Per the project's falsifiability rule, a position whose only remaining forms resist falsification is disqualified from physics-grade tiering regardless of its proponents' intent — this is the central reason it sits at E3 (not E2) and is marked disputed rather than vetted-as-settled-heterodox-physics."
