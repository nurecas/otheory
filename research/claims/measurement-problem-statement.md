steelman: "The measurement problem can be stated as an inconsistent triad: (1) the quantum state is a complete description of a physical system, (2) the state always evolves by the linear, deterministic Schrödinger equation, and (3) measurements have single, determinate outcomes. Standard quantum mechanics asserts all three, yet unitary evolution of a system entangled with a measuring device generically yields a superposition of outcome-records, not one outcome — so the three cannot all hold. This is a genuine logical tension in the textbook formalism, not a confusion invented by philosophers."
strongest_objection: "Some physicists (instrumentalists, QBists, many Copenhagen readings) deny this is a problem at all: quantum mechanics is a tool for predicting measurement statistics, not a description of an observer-independent reality, so claim (1) was never intended and there is no inconsistency to resolve. That move is coherent but changes the subject — it makes QM silent on what happens, which is itself a substantive (and contested) philosophical commitment."
falsifiability: "The triad itself is a piece of mathematical/logical structure (given standard QM's postulates), not an empirical claim, so it is not 'falsifiable' in the experimental sense — what is falsifiable is which resolution (denying 1, 2, or 3) matches nature, which current experiments cannot yet distinguish (see many-worlds-interpretation, de-broglie-bohm-pilot-wave, grw-csl-objective-collapse claims)."

RED-TEAM NOTES (2026-07-07): Both sources verified. Myrvold's SEP entry is the standard reference and states the
triad essentially as given here (the exact three-way inconsistency is standard in the philosophy-of-physics
literature, traceable through Maudlin's well-known 1995 formulation as well). Bell's "Against 'Measurement'"
(Physics World 1990, reprinted in Speakable and Unspeakable) is a real, checkable primary source arguing exactly
this point from a working physicist's side. No fabrication, no misrepresentation. Tier confirmed E1: this is
uncontroversial among specialists as a *statement of the problem* — only the resolution is disputed, not whether
the tension exists given the standard postulates. This is E1 as logical/mathematical structure of an agreed-upon
formalism, not as an empirical result per se, but the claim text is careful to say so and doesn't overclaim
"falsifiable" for the triad itself, only for which resolution matches nature — this is the correct, narrow reading.

It directly undercuts any reading of quantum mechanics that treats "the observer" as settled physics with an
agreed meaning: the word "observer" in this triad names an unsolved gap, not a discovered mental faculty.
O Theory's move from "there is an observer effect" to "consciousness is fundamental" needs a specific
interpretation (E2-E4) to fill this gap — the triad alone licenses no such conclusion.

Steelman and strongest objection both hold up on inspection; no changes needed beyond confirming tier/status.
