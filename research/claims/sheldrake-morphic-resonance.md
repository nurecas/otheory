steelman: "Sheldrake, a credentialed biologist (Cambridge PhD), poses real open questions about biological form (morphogenesis, the limits of pure genetic/mechanistic explanation) and proposes a specific, stated hypothesis — that forms and behaviors are shaped by resonance with a 'morphic field' built from prior similar systems — framed as testable via experiments like remote staring detection, rat maze-learning speedups, and crystal-formation ease over time. Gomez-Marin (2021), a credentialed neuroscientist writing in a mainstream peer-reviewed journal (BioEssays), argues the hypothesis has faced 'dogmatic' dismissal rather than sustained empirical engagement, and that developmental biology genuinely has not fully explained how form (not just sequence) is specified — a real, open scientific question independent of whether Sheldrake's specific mechanism is right."
strongest_objection: "Independent replication attempts of Sheldrake's flagship experiments have failed: the most prominent (Richard Wiseman's replication of the staring-detection effect, and the Wiseman/Smith/Milton replication of the Jaytee dog-telepathy claim) found no effect beyond chance/ordinary explanation. The hypothesis posits an undetected field with no specified physical carrier, no measurable quantity, and no mechanism consistent with known physics (unlike electromagnetic or gravitational fields, which are directly measurable and obey stated equations), and mainstream physics and biology reject it as pseudoscience (Maddox 1981; general scientific consensus per the Wikipedia summary). A specific numeric claim sometimes repeated in popular sources — that '45 of 50' replication attempts failed — could not be traced to a citable primary meta-analysis during this review and should not be asserted as a precise, sourced statistic; the qualitative claim (the substantial majority of independent replications have failed or returned chance-level results) is well supported without it. Note also that Gomez-Marin (2021), the strongest pro-Sheldrake source located, itself concedes 'the experimental implications of his ideas remain largely untested' after 40 years — which cuts against, not for, treating morphic resonance as a well-tested-and-confirmed theory, and equally against overstating how thoroughly it has been 'tested and failed.' The honest summary is: the specific flagship experiments that have been independently tested (staring detection, Jaytee) failed; the broader field-mechanism claim remains untestable in the sense that matters (no independent physical detection of the posited field exists, and none is proposed)."
falsifiability: "Stated as testable by its proponent (staring detection, learning-rate transfer, crystallization rates), and the specific experiments that have been run have failed under independent replication (Wiseman et al., on both staring detection and Jaytee). As a general field-mechanism claim, however, it also fails on a deeper level because no independent physical detection method for the posited field is specified at all — there is nothing an experimenter could point an instrument at. This double failure (specific tests failed; general mechanism unfalsifiable) is why the hypothesis is tiered E6 rather than E3-with-heavy-caveats."

Morphic resonance is filed E6: a fringe hypothesis presenting explicitly as biological science, proposed by a credentialed researcher, whose specific flagship experiments have failed independent replication, and which posits an undetected, unmeasurable field with no specified physical carrier. Relevant to O Theory's "resonance/field" imagery as a cautionary case: this is the specific named hypothesis that such imagery risks being mistaken for, and it does not pass vetting as physics.

**Red-team notes (2026-07-07) — tier re-examined, citation corrected, kept at E6:**

*Citation error found and fixed:* the draft attributed the 2021 BioEssays retrospective to "Freeman, A. (2021)." I confirmed via PubMed, the publisher (Wiley/BioEssays), and independent search that the actual author is **Alex Gomez-Marin**, not Freeman — there is no "Freeman, A." author on this paper. The PMID (33751607) and DOI were correct, so this was a misattribution rather than a fabricated source, but it needed correcting before this file could be marked verified. Fixed in the sources list above.

*Statistical claim flagged as unverifiable:* the draft's assertion that "of roughly 50 attempted replications catalogued across four decades, the substantial majority (about 45) failed to reproduce the claimed effects" could not be traced to a citable primary source during this review (it appears in some secondary blog/aggregator material online but not in the cited Wikipedia article or in any peer-reviewed meta-analysis I could locate). I have removed the specific "45 of 50" figure from the strongest_objection and replaced it with the qualitative, well-supported claim (substantial majority of independent replications failed), sourced to the concrete, checkable cases Wikipedia does document (Wiseman's staring-detection replication, and the Wiseman/Smith/Milton Jaytee replication). This is a case of a plausible-sounding statistic that should not have been asserted without a primary source — flagging per the "never launder evidence" directive even though it worked in the claim's favor.

*E3-vs-E6 re-examination (per instruction to specifically re-examine this call):* The case for E3-heavy-caveats would be: credentialed proponent (Cambridge PhD biologist), a genuine open question in developmental biology (how form, not just molecular sequence, is specified — the "genome wager" problem also referenced in adjacent literature), and a peer-reviewed 2021 retrospective (Gomez-Marin, BioEssays) arguing the hypothesis has been dismissed rather than rigorously tested. Weighed against this: (1) the specific, named flagship experiments Sheldrake himself proposed as tests (staring detection, Jaytee telepathic dog) HAVE been independently run and HAVE failed under replication — this is not an untested hypothesis in the areas where it made concrete predictions; (2) the general "morphic field" mechanism specifies no physical carrier, no equations, and no proposed method of detection even in principle — it fails the falsifiability rule not because no one tried, but because nothing is stated that could be tried; (3) mainstream biology and physics reject it as pseudoscience, and this is not merely majority-opinion bias — no alternative operationalization of "morphic field" consistent with known physics has been offered by anyone, including Sheldrake, in over 40 years. On balance this remains E6: it presents explicitly as an empirical, testable scientific hypothesis (not a philosophical position, which would push toward E4), its specific tests failed, and its general mechanism is unfalsifiable in principle, not just untested in practice. The genuine open question in developmental biology that motivates it is real and worth noting (as done in the steelman) but is a separate claim from the specific mechanism, which fails. E3 is reserved for contested-but-mechanistically-stated minority positions (e.g., Orch-OR proposes a specific, if disputed, physical mechanism); Sheldrake's hypothesis does not clear that bar. Tier E6 confirmed, status rejected.
