steelman: "Modern physics (quantum statistics' violation of the classical individuality of particles, gauge theory's redundant descriptions of 'the same' physical state, general relativity's diffeomorphism invariance eroding a well-defined notion of 'the same point of spacetime' across models) is arguably incompatible with a metaphysics of self-identical individual objects bearing intrinsic properties; Ladyman and Ross argue we should instead take the relational, structural content that physics actually secures — and only that — as what exists, dissolving 'objects' into nodes defined purely by their relations."
strongest_objection: "OSR faces the charge of being unstable or even incoherent: relations are ordinarily understood as relations BETWEEN things, so a 'structure' with no relata risks being an empty formalism rather than an ontology (the 'Newman problem' lineage: critics ask what, if anything, stands in the relations); Ladyman and Ross's own reply — that they reject a purely extensional/set-theoretic reading of relations in favor of modal/causal ones — is itself contested as adding back exactly the sort of intrinsic, non-purely-relational structure (modal facts about what would happen) that was supposed to have been eliminated."

Ontic structural realism holds that reality is, at bottom, relational structure rather than a collection of individual objects that happen to stand in relations — a naturalistic metaphysics built by taking seriously what our best physics (quantum mechanics, quantum field theory, general relativity) is claimed to actually commit us to. It is relevant to O Theory's substrate claims insofar as it offers a rigorous, physics-engaged precedent for "relations/information, not stuff, is fundamental" without invoking consciousness — a genuinely different route to a similar anti-substance conclusion from process philosophy. Tiered E4: it is a metaphysical reading of physics (what physical theories are ultimately about), argued philosophically, not a further empirical claim that physics itself could confirm or refute.
