steelman: "The Principle of Sufficient Reason (PSR), articulated centrally by Leibniz and (per many scholars, preceding him) Spinoza, generalizes the demand for explanation that drives all rational and scientific inquiry (every observed fact is presumed to have a cause or reason) to the totality of facts, including contingent existence itself; taken seriously it motivates cosmological arguments for a self-explaining necessary being/ground and, more modestly, licenses treating 'why is there something rather than nothing' as a legitimate rather than confused question, one that any candidate fundamental-substrate theory (physical, informational, or theological) is implicitly attempting to answer."
strongest_objection: "Strong forms of PSR arguably entail counterintuitive or unpalatable consequences — the SEP entry notes that for Spinoza, PSR arguably 'dictates that there is only one possible world' (necessitarianism: everything, including apparently contingent facts, is necessary) — or an infinite regress of reasons; many philosophers hold the 'something rather than nothing' question may be malformed or unanswerable in principle, since any candidate explanation must itself invoke some existing thing or principle, begging the question of why THAT exists rather than nothing. This objection applies with equal force to every candidate 'fundamental substrate' theory, including O Theory's own posited O-Source: positing a substrate as brute/necessary does not itself satisfy PSR unless one can also explain why that particular substrate, rather than no substrate or a different one, is the necessary one — a demand no substrate monism (physical, informational, mathematical, or experiential) has been shown to meet."
falsifiability: "Not empirically decidable — a metaphysical/logical principle debated by conceptual argument (does every fact require a reason; is the question of ultimate existence well-formed), with no experiment able to adjudicate it."

The Principle of Sufficient Reason holds that every fact demands an explanation, which extended to the universe's existence itself yields the classical question "why is there something rather than nothing" (Leibniz; popularized as, per Heidegger, "the most fundamental issue of philosophy"). This is the deepest-level framing question any "fundamental substrate" proposal — physical, informational, mathematical, or experiential — is implicitly answering, including O Theory's O-Source; filed as its own claim because it names the demand for ultimate explanation, distinct from any particular candidate answer (matter, mind, information, mathematics) filed elsewhere in this domain. Tier E4: whether the PSR holds at all, and whether the "something rather than nothing" question is even well-posed, are contested philosophical questions with no empirical test.
