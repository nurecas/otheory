steelman: "For Hilbert space dimension >= 3, no assignment of definite pre-existing values to all quantum observables can be made that (a) is noncontextual (a value doesn't depend on which other compatible measurements are made alongside it) and (b) reproduces quantum mechanics' functional/orthogonality constraints. This is a proven, purely mathematical result about the structure of quantum observables, subsequently confirmed experimentally (state-independent contextuality tests with trapped ions and photons) — E1, both as theorem and as verified prediction."
strongest_objection: "The theorem constrains noncontextual hidden-variable models; it does not touch contextual hidden-variable theories (Bohmian mechanics is explicitly contextual and survives), nor does it by itself say anything about consciousness or observers in the everyday sense — 'context' here means the full set of jointly-measured compatible operators, a technical notion of experimental arrangement, not a mind. It is frequently misappropriated in popular writing as showing 'reality depends on the observer's mind,' which overstates a result about measurement-context-dependence into a claim about mental causation."
falsifiability: "As a mathematical theorem it is proven, not falsifiable; its empirical counterpart (that real quantum systems exhibit contextual, not noncontextual-hidden-variable, statistics) has been tested and confirmed (Kirchmair et al. 2009 and later experiments) and would be falsified by any noncontextual-compatible dataset, none has been found."
