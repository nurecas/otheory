steelman: "GRT proposes that when physical constituents share a resonant/synchronized frequency of energy-information exchange, they combine into a richer unified system with a correspondingly richer (or more basic, at simple scales) form of experience, offering a candidate physical marker (shared resonance/synchrony) for when and how micro-experiences combine into macro-experiences — directly addressing panpsychism's notorious 'combination problem.' It draws support by analogy from known physical synchronization phenomena (coupled oscillators, brain-wave phase synchrony correlating with binding/integration in neuroscience) and has been published in peer-reviewed venues (Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, Journal of Consciousness Studies) with growing citation."
strongest_objection: "GRT's core move — that synchronized resonance is what 'binds' micro-experience into macro-experience — is stated at the level of a suggestive analogy to known physics (oscillator synchronization) rather than as a derived, quantitatively predictive mechanism; it does not specify a measurable resonance signature that would be present in a conscious system and absent in a merely synchronized non-conscious one (many non-conscious physical systems, e.g., coupled pendulum clocks or power-grid generators, synchronize without any consciousness attribution, and GRT's proponents have not given a principled criterion excluding these). It inherits the panpsychist framework's foundational commitment (all matter has some proto-experience) which is itself a philosophical, not empirical, premise, and this micro-to-macro combination story is a minority position even within panpsychism, which has its own unresolved internal debates about the combination problem. No experiment has been proposed that would distinguish GRT's predictions from ordinary neural-synchrony correlates of consciousness (already well documented in NCC research) explained without any panpsychist commitment. Hence E4: an intriguing philosophical proposal building on real physics vocabulary (resonance, synchrony) but not itself empirically testable as a theory of experience, and 'disputed' even among philosophers of mind sympathetic to panpsychism generally, not just skeptics of panpsychism."
falsifiability: "Not clearly falsifiable as a theory of WHY resonance yields experience (any observed synchrony correlate of consciousness is consistent with GRT, but so is a purely functional/computational account of the same synchrony data, with no way to adjudicate). Narrower sub-claims about which resonance frequencies correlate with reported experience are empirically testable and overlap with mainstream NCC/oscillation research; the panpsychist metaphysical core is not testable. Classified E4."

A minority philosophical position that uses real physical vocabulary (resonance, phase synchrony) metaphorically/analogically to address the combination problem for panpsychism, not a mainstream or experimentally supported theory of consciousness. Relevant to O Theory's own resonance/frequency language (e.g., any "bliss attractor as a resonance state" framing) as the closest existing academic analogue — the connection between GRT and any such framing should be labeled analogy, not shared-mathematics, since GRT itself has not derived a quantitative resonance criterion for consciousness.

Red-team note (2026-07-07): both sources verified — Hunt & Schooler 2019 Frontiers in Human Neuroscience article confirmed (added volume/article number 13:378), and the JCS 2020 follow-up confirmed as a real published piece. Status changed to "disputed" to reflect that GRT is a minority position even within the (already minority) panpsychist research community, not merely disputed by materialist critics — this should NOT be read by O Theory's synthesis as an established or even majority panpsychist account of the combination problem. The claim's own caution about "analogy, not shared-mathematics" for any resonance-language borrowing is exactly right and should be enforced strictly.
