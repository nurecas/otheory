steelman: "Kabbalistic theosophy (systematized from 13th-century Provence and Spain, culminating in the Zohar) holds that Ein Sof ('the infinite'), the hidden godhead beyond all attributes and thus beyond comprehension, manifests the finite, structured world through ten sefirot — dynamic divine attributes/emanations (e.g. Ḥokhmah/wisdom, Binah/understanding, Tiferet/beauty) arranged in the 'Tree of Life' — providing a rich symbolic-theological account of how an unknowable absolute relates to a differentiated cosmos without simple identity or simple separation. As SEP notes, Kabbalists themselves (e.g. Rabbi Azriel of Gerona) argued the sefirot exist 'in potentia' within Ein Sof and share its power, precisely to defend the doctrine's monotheism against charges of introducing multiplicity into God."
strongest_objection: "Historically, mainstream rabbinic and rationalist Jewish philosophy (e.g. Maimonidean strands) treated much of this theosophical elaboration with suspicion as verging on multiplicity within the Godhead, and per SEP some Kabbalists' talk of 'three hidden lights' drew explicit accusations of crypto-Trinitarianism from critics; the framework is explicitly esoteric/symbolic and its claims about Ein Sof are, by its own account, not literally describable, making empirical adjudication moot by design."

Kabbalistic thought holds that God's innermost, hidden aspect, Ein Sof ("without end"), is infinite and utterly without attributes, and therefore in itself incomprehensible; the finite, structured cosmos and the personal God of scripture emerge through ten sefirot, stages of divine self-manifestation depicted as a "Tree of Life" connecting the hidden and the revealed. Represented here as Kabbalah's own symbolic-theological claim (E5); any O-Theory analogy between Ein Sof and an "O-Source" is metaphor only — the sefirotic structure is a theological-symbolic diagram, not a physical or mathematical model, and should not be presented as either.

Red-team notes (translation-rights / attribution flag): Confirmed the Nurho de Manhar Zohar translation hosted at sacred-texts.com is (a) by a pseudonymous, probably-British Theosophist whose real identity is unknown, (b) covers only the first ~200 pages of the Zohar (Bereshith through Lekh Lekha), (c) uses idiosyncratic Theosophical terminology ("Karma," "Planes of Existence") that does not map cleanly onto classical Kabbalistic categories, and (d) is explicitly flagged by the hosting archive's own editor as not recommended as an introductory text. This is exactly the kind of non-critical, pseudonymous translation the vetting brief warned about. Fix applied: retained the source (it is a real, legitimately public-domain text, not fabricated) but demoted its role in the source list to "illustrative color, not load-bearing," and made the load-bearing account rest on the SEP "Monotheism" §7.1 secondary source instead, which is independently confirmed to substantively and accurately cover Ein Sof and the sefirot (including Rabbi Azriel of Gerona's in potentia defense against the multiplicity charge, now folded into the steelman). No flattening detected — the claim correctly refuses to treat the sefirot as physical/energetic.
