The Trikāya ("three bodies") doctrine, developed in Mahāyāna Buddhism from sources including the Lotus Sūtra, describes Buddhahood as having three aspects: the Dharmakāya (the unconditioned "truth body," the formless ground of enlightenment itself, out of which Buddhas arise and to which they return), the Saṃbhogakāya (a "body of bliss" perceptible to advanced beings), and the Nirmāṇakāya (the physical body that appears in history, e.g. Śākyamuni). Represented here as Mahāyāna's own doctrinal claim (E5); any O-Theory analogy between Dharmakāya and an "O-Source" ground-of-being is metaphor only, not a shared structure, and notably the Dharmakāya is explicitly bound up with Buddhology (the nature of Buddhahood) rather than being a general cosmological substrate.
