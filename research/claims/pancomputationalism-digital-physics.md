steelman: "If every fundamental physical magnitude and its dynamics can, without approximation, be exactly captured by some computational formalism (a cellular automaton or a quantum-computational network), then 'computation' can be taken as the more fundamental description, with 'matter' and 'field' as higher-level readings of that computational structure; this motivates concrete research programs (e.g. Wolfram's cellular-automaton models, 't Hooft's cellular-automaton interpretation of quantum mechanics)."
strongest_objection: "Ontic pancomputationalism conflates a description with the thing described: that a system's dynamics can be exactly modeled by a computation does not establish that the system IS a computation rather than a physical process that happens to be computationally simulable. The SEP entry (Piccinini, section 3.4) makes this exact point and additionally reports 'little positive evidence' for the ontic (as opposed to purely epistemic/modeling) version of the thesis, plus the general objection that nearly any sufficiently rich formalism can be shown 'equivalent' to some computational one, threatening to make the ontic claim empirically empty."
falsifiability: "As a strict identity claim ('reality just is computation'), not falsifiable — a metaphysical interpretation layered onto uncontested mathematical facts about which formalisms can simulate which. Narrower empirical versions (e.g., specific discreteness-of-spacetime predictions from a given cellular-automaton model) could in principle be tested and are pursued as heterodox physics (E3), but the general ontic thesis remains E4 and is explicitly flagged here as not currently falsifiable."

Pancomputationalism generalizes "it from bit" into the stronger, more literal thesis that the universe's fundamental dynamics simply are a computation (digital physics), most concretely developed via cellular-automaton models. It is filed as a distinct claim from Wheeler's "it from bit" because it makes a stronger and more specific ontological/formal claim (computation as literal substrate, with named candidate formalisms) rather than a looser information-primacy slogan. Relevant to O Theory's "information as substrate" framing, but must be kept E4: the identity of "physics" with "computation" is an interpretive stance about formalism-equivalence, not a demonstrated or falsified empirical result.
