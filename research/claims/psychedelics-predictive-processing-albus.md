steelman: "REBUS proposed that serotonergic psychedelics (via 5-HT2A agonism) reduce the precision-weighting ('relax') of high-level prior beliefs, flattening the brain's predictive hierarchy and explaining increased entropy, ego-dissolution, and openness to revised beliefs — a model with independent support from reduced gamma power and increased signal entropy under psychedelics, and clinical correlations between acute belief-relaxation and later symptom improvement in depression trials. The 2025 ALBUS update, integrating newer data, argues low-to-moderate doses often instead strengthen (not relax) beliefs at intermediate hierarchical levels (producing vivid meaning-laden imagery, pareidolia, and 'waking dream' phenomenology), giving a more complete dose- and context-dependent account consistent with both classic ego-dissolution reports and lower-dose 'enhanced meaning' reports."
strongest_objection: "Both models operate largely at the level of theoretical neural-process narrative fitted post hoc to phenomenological reports and a limited set of neuroimaging findings (chiefly entropy/complexity measures and some connectivity changes); the specific mechanistic claims (precision-weighting of priors at specific cortical hierarchy levels, hippocampal/entorhinal remapping) are, by the ALBUS authors' own admission, mostly untested directly — most animal pharmacology uses a crude 'head-twitch' behavioral proxy rather than characterizing graded dose-response belief effects. A further, domain-general confound applies to nearly all human psychedelic-trial phenomenology cited in support of either model: functional unblinding. Subjects and raters in psychedelic studies can almost always tell active drug from placebo because of the drug's obvious acute subjective and physiological effects, undermining the blinding that clinical/cognitive claims about belief-updating depend on — a criticism increasingly raised across the psychedelic-science literature independent of REBUS/ALBUS specifically. The free-energy/predictive-processing framework underlying both models has itself been criticized as difficult to falsify (see predictive-processing-free-energy-principle). The models are genuine, actively-developed neuroscience of altered states, but remain interpretive frameworks fitted to correlational, functionally-unblinded data, not confirmed mechanistic accounts — E3, and REBUS vs. ALBUS is itself an active, unresolved dispute within the field (hence 'disputed' status), not a settled successor relationship."
falsifiability: "Partially: some sub-claims are testable (e.g., dose-dependent EEG entropy/complexity changes, gamma-power reduction, correlating acute subjective belief-flexibility with clinical outcome) and have preliminary support; others (precision-weighting at specific hierarchical levels, the proposed inverted-U dose-response for cognitive stability) are not yet directly tested, which the ALBUS authors themselves flag as a gap. Functional unblinding limits how decisively any of these correlational human studies can adjudicate the underlying computational claims."

This is an empirical/theoretical neuroscience claim about the mechanism of psychedelic action, distinct from any further metaphysical reading (e.g., that psychedelic states reveal a "true," normally-filtered non-local reality — an E4 interpretation not filed as its own claim here but relevant to the same observation/interpretation split governing this domain). Both REBUS and ALBUS are brain-based, materialist models of altered belief-updating; they do not themselves make claims about non-local consciousness, and should not be read as supporting O Theory's filtration framing without that further, separately-tiered step.
