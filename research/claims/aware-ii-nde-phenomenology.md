steelman: "This is a genuine multi-center prospective study (567 cardiac-arrest patients, 25 hospitals, US/UK, 2017–2020): of the 53 survivors (9.3%), 28 (52.8%) completed interviews, and 11 of those 28 (39.3%) reported memories or perceptions suggestive of awareness during the arrest, described as qualitatively distinct from ordinary hallucination, delusion, illusion, dream, or drug-induced (CPR-induced) consciousness by the study's own classification. In a subset with EEG monitoring, the study documents normal-appearing delta/theta/alpha activity re-emerging as long as 35–60 minutes into CPR despite severe measured cerebral hypoxia (mean regional oxygen saturation ~43%), which the authors present as evidence against the assumption that the brain is uniformly electrically silent throughout arrest."
strongest_objection: "The self-reported-experience finding rests on a small, retrospective-interview subsample (28 of 567) with obvious survivorship and recall biases, no way to timestamp reported content against physiological events, and a single implicit-learning auditory-stimulus test in which only 1 of 28 patients (3.5%) correctly identified the stimulus and zero identified the visual image — a near-chance null result for objective verification of perception during arrest. The EEG finding (organized delta/theta/alpha reappearing under hypoxia) is real and worth explaining — confirmed as reported (interpretable EEG was obtained from only 53 of 567 patients, roughly half showing near-normal activity) — but 'normal-appearing background rhythms during CPR' is not itself evidence of conscious content; correlating specific EEG signatures with the timing of specific recalled content was not achieved. This is a suggestive, methodologically constrained observation, not a demonstration that structured conscious experience occurs during confirmed cardiac standstill — E3, heavily caveated."
falsifiability: "Yes, and it is an active program: better time-locked EEG/near-infrared monitoring paired with implicit-learning target stimuli (as AWARE-II attempted, with a null result so far) could in principle establish whether reportable perception is occurring during measured brain hypoperfusion, versus being a memory constructed at the moment of or after recovery of full function."

This is the OBSERVATION claim: what AWARE-II actually measured and reported (recall rates, EEG activity during CPR, phenomenological categories), independent of any survival/non-local-mind reading. It is explicitly split from aware-ii-nde-survival-interpretation (E4), which treats the phenomenology as evidence of a mind operating independent of, or surviving, brain function. The study authors' own "disinhibition" hypothesis (removal of inhibitory braking systems opening access to stored memory) is itself a materialist, brain-based speculative mechanism, and is noted here as their preferred but unconfirmed explanation — distinct from stronger survival claims layered on top by others.

Red-team note (2026-07-07): source verified directly against the PubMed abstract — all figures confirmed exactly (567 total arrests, 53 survivors/9.3%, 28 interviews/52.8%, 11/28 = 39.3% reporting memories, 1/28 = 3.5% correct on auditory implicit-learning test, 0/28 on visual, delta/theta/alpha EEG activity 35-60 min into CPR at mean rSO2 43%). This is an exceptionally well-sourced and precisely stated claim. Tier E3 with heavy caveats is correct and, if anything, this claim undersells how weak the implicit-learning null result is for anyone tempted to read this as verified perception during confirmed brain inactivity — it is worth emphasizing in synthesis writing that the ONE objective test of perception during arrest failed almost completely (near chance).
