steelman: "Hood's 32-item M-Scale (1975) was built directly to operationalize eight of Stace's nine phenomenological criteria, and the 2001 cross-cultural confirmatory factor analysis (a mostly-Christian US sample, N=1,379, replicated in a second US sample, N=188, and an Iranian Muslim sample, N=185) replicated a stable three-factor structure separating general mystical phenomenology (introvertive/extrovertive) from its religious interpretation — a non-trivial empirical result suggesting the *phenomenology* factor and the *doctrinal-interpretation* factor are statistically separable even when the interpreting religions differ sharply. This is real cross-cultural psychometric evidence bearing on (though not settling) the perennialism debate."
strongest_objection: "Factor replication across two culture samples that both used the same translated instrument does not establish that the underlying experiences are culture-independent — it may show only that the same questionnaire, once translated, elicits self-consistent answer patterns, which is a weaker and different claim than 'the same experience occurs regardless of culture.' This objection is sharpened by the study's own finding: the relationship between the phenomenology factors and the interpretation factor was NOT identical across cultures — the American sample showed a stronger association between the two phenomenology factors than the Iranian sample did — meaning the 'stable three-factor structure' claim is true at the level of which items group together, but the strength of the underlying relationships between factors already varies by culture, a fact the steelman should not smooth over. The scale is also validated mainly in Abrahamic/Islamic populations to date; broader non-theistic (e.g., Theravada Buddhist, secular) validation is thinner. As with the MEQ30, self-report cannot adjudicate the metaphysical content of the reported unity, and the same Stace-derived item-circularity risk applies here as with the MEQ30."
falsifiability: "Empirical/psychometric claim: falsifiable via factor-analytic replication, cross-cultural measurement invariance testing, and predictive/convergent validity against other measures. Correctly tiered E3: a real, replicated but scope-limited empirical result about self-report structure, not a metaphysical claim about what mystical experience reveals."

This is an EMPIRICAL MEASUREMENT claim (E3): Hood's M-Scale takes Stace's E5 typology and turns it into a testable self-report instrument, and cross-cultural factor analyses (US/Iran) found a replicable structure separating raw phenomenology from religious interpretation. This is evidence about how people's *reports* cluster statistically — it is not evidence for or against the perennialism-constructivism debate's metaphysical stakes (filed separately) nor for any claim about a mind-independent unitive reality. Useful to O Theory only as a measurement layer showing the reported phenomenology is structured and repeatable, not as support for the O-Source interpretation.

**Red-team notes (2026-07-07):** Both sources verified — Hood 1975 confirmed (Journal for the Scientific Study of Religion 14:29-41; 32-item M-Scale built on Stace's 1960 categories, factor analysis into general-mysticism and religious-interpretation factors, matching the claim's description); Hood, Ghorbani et al. 2001 confirmed via Wiley abstract and independent search, with exact sample sizes (US N=1,379 confirmatory sample, second US N=188, Iran N=185) recoverable — added to the steelman for precision since the draft only said "US Christian sample and an Iranian Muslim sample" without N's. Tier E3 correct. Materially strengthened the objection: independent search surfaced that the 2001 paper itself reports the phenomenology-interpretation relationship was NOT identical across the two cultures (Americans showed a stronger phenomenology-factor intercorrelation), which cuts against reading this as unqualified "the same structure replicates across cultures" — the draft steelman said "stable three-factor structure...replicated" without flagging that the factor *structure* (which items load where) replicated while a *relationship* between factors did not fully replicate. This nuance matters for the perennialism debate and is now explicit rather than smoothed over.
