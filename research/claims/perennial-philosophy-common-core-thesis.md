steelman: "Perennialists (Huxley 1945; Schuon 1975 in his 'Traditionalist' formulation; Stace's related essentialism about experience) argue that beneath radically different doctrines, symbols, and cultures, contemplatives across traditions converge on structurally similar reports — e.g. Stace's 'introvertive' experience of undifferentiated 'pure consciousness' or unity — suggesting either a shared underlying metaphysical reality or at least a universal structure of the contemplative mind, and this comparative pattern-matching has been taken seriously by scholars of religion (not only popularizers)."
strongest_objection: "Steven Katz's constructivist critique (1978, 'Language, Epistemology, and Mysticism') is the dominant scholarly rebuttal, and per SEP represents a 'hard constructivism' holding that a mystic's prior conceptual scheme completely determines the nature and content of the experience, not merely its post-hoc interpretation: a Christian's unio mystica and a Buddhist's realization of śūnyatā are, on this view, different experiences shaped differently by different traditions, not one experience under different names. Per SEP, we only ever have mystics' post-experiential, already-interpreted reports (there is no access to a 'raw' pre-linguistic experience to compare across traditions), making the underlying-sameness claim empirically and hermeneutically unresolvable either way — though SEP also notes counter-objections to hard constructivism (e.g. reports of 'thin descriptions' prior to elaborate cultural framing, and meditative disciplines that aim to strip away cultural content), so the debate remains genuinely live, not settled in Katz's favor either."
falsifiability: "Not empirically decidable even in principle (we cannot access pre-linguistic 'raw' mystical experience to compare) → E4 (philosophical/comparative-hermeneutic claim), shading into E5 when stated as the traditions' own self-understanding. Treating 'they all say the same thing' as an established fact would launder a contested comparative thesis into settled truth. No experiment could adjudicate between the essentialist and constructivist readings of the same reports, which is precisely why this stays E4 rather than becoming E1/E2."

The perennial philosophy thesis, given canonical form by Aldous Huxley (1945) and developed in stronger metaphysical ("Traditionalist") form by Frithjof Schuon, claims that the world's major mystical and contemplative traditions point to (or report) the same non-dual ultimate reality beneath surface doctrinal differences. This is a contested comparative-religion/philosophical meta-claim (E4), not an established fact: the dominant scholarly counter-position, Steven Katz's constructivism, holds that mystical experience is culturally and conceptually mediated all the way down, so apparent convergence may reflect comparative flattening rather than a shared reality. O Theory's use of Brahman/Dharmakāya/the One/Ein Sof etc. as analogues for an "O-Source" leans on this perennialist reading; that reliance should be flagged explicitly as adopting one side of a live, unresolved scholarly debate, not as citing a consensus.

Scope note (kept distinct from a related claim): this claim is the comparative-religion *metaphysical* thesis — do the traditions' doctrines/ultimates (Brahman, śūnyatā, the One, Ein Sof, Dao, Godhead, wujūd, spanda, Dharmakāya) refer to the same one reality. It is distinct from, though closely related to and cross-referenced with, `perennialism-constructivism-debate` in the mysticism domain, which concerns the *epistemology of mystical experience itself* (whether first-person contemplative reports are raw/universal or conceptually constructed all the way down). The two claims should not be merged: one is about traditions' referents, the other about the nature of the experiential reports used as evidence for those referents — though Katz's constructivism is the shared fulcrum of both debates and is cited in both.
