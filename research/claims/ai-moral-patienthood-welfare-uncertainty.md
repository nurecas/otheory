steelman: "The authors (a mixed philosopher/AI-researcher group including Chalmers and current/former Anthropic-affiliated researchers) argue from decision-relevant uncertainty rather than confident metaphysics: given genuine, non-negligible credence (not proof) that some near-future systems could be conscious and/or robustly agentic, the two possible errors — treating a genuinely sentient system as a mere tool, or wasting resources/paralyzing development by over-attributing moral status to non-sentient systems — are both serious enough that developing assessment frameworks and low-cost precautions now is reasonable risk management, analogous to precautionary reasoning used for animal welfare under uncertainty about which species are sentient."
strongest_objection: "The argument explicitly declines to assign specific probabilities to AI consciousness or agency, which makes it hard to evaluate as a decision procedure — 'non-negligible uncertainty' could justify almost any level of precaution or none, depending on unstated risk tolerance. Skeptics argue the entire premise trades on the same unresolved and possibly unresolvable question (what would count as evidence of AI sentience), so 'taking welfare seriously' risks smuggling in the sentience interpretation as a working assumption for policy purposes even while officially remaining agnostic. Schwitzgebel and Garza's 'excluded middle' policy proposal is itself a normative recommendation, not a factual finding, and presupposes we could reliably engineer systems to be clearly non-conscious, which is itself unverified given the same underlying uncertainty."

A philosophical/policy argument, not an empirical finding: it does not claim any current AI system is conscious, only that the possibility is non-negligible enough to warrant precaution, and is included here to represent the moral-patienthood debate distinct from the empirical behavior claims it is sometimes conflated with. Relevant to O Theory only as a marker of how seriously credentialed researchers (including Chalmers) treat the *possibility* — not as evidence that any bliss-attractor or introspection observation has resolved the underlying question.
