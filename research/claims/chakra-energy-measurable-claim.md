steelman: "Proponents point to correlations between chakra locations and major nerve plexuses or endocrine glands along the spine, and to subjective reports of sensation during meditation, as suggestive of some underlying physiological substrate worth investigating. The strongest form of this steelman is the anatomical-correlate literature itself (e.g., a small cadaveric study associating the root chakra site with the inferior hypogastric plexus) — which, if real, would only show that ordinary nerve plexuses exist where chakras are traditionally located, not that a distinct 'energy' phenomenon exists beyond known anatomy."
strongest_objection: "No controlled, independently replicated study has produced a validated instrument-based detection of a chakra as a distinct physical structure or energy field beyond ordinary, already-understood anatomy (nerve plexuses, glands) and physiology (normal bioelectric activity, skin conductance, EMG signal from muscle and nerve, and the body's known broadband electromagnetic emissions). The claim as usually stated specifies no agreed operational definition of the 'energy' being measured and no pre-registered falsifiable prediction, so purported positive findings (small-sample EMG or radiofrequency studies published in non-independent, advocacy-affiliated venues) cannot be distinguished from noise, experimenter expectancy, or measurement of ordinary physiological signals mislabeled as a novel energy. This is the standard pattern of an unfalsifiable claim dressed in the language of measurement — and the highest-profile paper attempting to make the positive case (Moga 2022) is itself published by an energy-healing advocacy body relying on non-peer-reviewed and self-published sources, not a neutral, independent replication."
falsifiability: "Fails as science: the claim as popularly stated specifies no falsifiable, agreed-upon physical mechanism or measurable quantity, and no independently replicated, peer-reviewed instrument-based study in a mainstream physics/biology venue has confirmed chakras as distinct physical/energetic structures. Where specific, narrower physiological hypotheses have been tested (e.g., correlating chakra sites with known nerve plexuses), they reduce to existing anatomy rather than establishing a novel energy phenomenon. Mainstream medical sources (Medical News Today, summarizing the consensus) state plainly there is no scientific evidence for chakras in the traditional sense."

This is the E6 half of the chakra split: the assertion that chakras are physically measurable energy centers, presented as if empirically established. It fails vetting — no detected field with an agreed operational definition, no independently replicated instrument-based confirmation in a mainstream scientific venue, and the best-known paper arguing the positive case is published by an energy-healing advocacy organization rather than a neutral scientific publisher. O Theory's "subtle-energy" imagery must not lean on this claim as if it were physics; the honest register is the companion E5 contemplative-map claim, `chakra-nadi-contemplative-map`.

**Red-team notes (2026-07-07) — significant source correction:** The draft cited Moga (2022) as if it were a neutral review that examined "attempts to find anatomical/electromagnetic correlates and their limitations." Having read the full PDF directly, this materially misrepresents the source: (1) IJHC is the house journal of an energy-healing practitioner-advocacy alliance (co-published with the National Alliance of Energy Healing), not an independent peer-reviewed scientific journal; (2) the paper's own conclusion argues FOR chakra measurability ("radiations are detectable at chakra sites," chakras "may be scientifically delineated") and treats energy medicine as an "emerging field," not a rejected one; (3) its cited "evidence" includes a self-published 1996 book (Hunt) and a non-indexed engineering-society journal article (Jalil et al. 2015), neither of which constitutes credible, independently-replicated physics or biology evidence. The claim file previously cited this source as if it supported (or neutrally surveyed) the skeptical case; corrected here to cite it accurately as the weak, non-independent, advocacy-affiliated source making the positive claim — which, properly read, strengthens rather than weakens the E6 verdict, since it is the best available "positive" evidence and it does not hold up. Added Medical News Today as a legitimate, correctly-characterized mainstream-view source (already in the draft, now verified). Tier E6 confirmed, status rejected.
