steelman: "Alfred Watkins's original 1921 observation — that some ancient landmarks in the English landscape appear to line up on maps — was an honest empirical observation about mapped points, and general claims that sound/music affect mood and physiology are well supported (music therapy has real, measured effects). The steelman is limited strictly to these narrower, checkable observations."
strongest_objection: "The straight-line alignments are statistically expected by chance given the high density of historic sites in landscapes like Britain (critics including archaeologist O.G.S. Crawford noted this at the time), and the subsequent claim that such lines carry a detectable 'earth energy' has never been measured by any instrument or shown to have a physical carrier. Likewise, claims that a specific frequency (432 Hz, or 'Solfeggio' frequencies) has unique healing, DNA-repairing, or toxin-removing properties are not supported by controlled studies; Hertz as a unit postdates any claimed ancient use, and comparative 432-vs-440 Hz studies show no consistent effect beyond ordinary music-preference variation. Both are cases of a real, narrow observation (map alignments; music affects mood) inflated into an unfalsifiable energy claim — no mechanism, no instrument-based detection, no controlled study showing an effect beyond chance or ordinary psychoacoustics."
falsifiability: "Fails as science: no accepted instrument detects 'ley energy,' and controlled studies of specific healing frequencies find no effect beyond general, frequency-independent effects of music/sound already explained by ordinary psychoacoustics. The narrow claims (map alignments exist; sound affects mood) are true but do not support the broader energetic/pseudoscientific claim layered onto them, and nothing is specified that could falsify the 'earth energy' or 'unique healing frequency' claims as usually stated — which is itself disqualifying."

Ley lines and "vibration/frequency" healing claims are grouped as one E6 pattern: a real, narrow observation (landscape alignments; music affects listeners) inflated, without evidence or mechanism, into a claimed detectable "energy" or uniquely privileged frequency. Both fail vetting for the same reason — no instrument-based detection, no controlled supporting study, no physical mechanism. Directly relevant to any "resonance/frequency" language O Theory borrows aesthetically: it must not imply this literature supports a physical claim.
