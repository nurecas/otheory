steelman: "Wigner catalogs concrete historical cases — complex numbers turning out to be essential to quantum mechanics, Riemannian geometry (developed with no physical motivation) turning out to be exactly the mathematics of general relativity, group representation theory anticipating particle spectra — where mathematics invented for its own internal reasons was later found to fit nature with precision far beyond what was built into it. This pattern, as a historical/sociological observation about the practice of physics, is well documented and widely discussed among physicists and mathematicians."
strongest_objection: "The observation itself is at most a documented historical pattern (E1, as an empirical claim about the history of science), not a proof of anything about the ultimate nature of mathematics or reality. Wigner's essay reports a genuine and widely discussed pattern in the history of physics (E1 as an observation): mathematics developed independently of empirical motivation has repeatedly proven exactly suited to describing nature. This claim is deliberately kept separate from `mathematical-platonism`, the metaphysical interpretation often drawn from it — the observation is evidential and historical, the Platonist explanation is one philosophical position among several, and CLAUDE.md requires they not be collapsed into one another. O Theory's "unreasonable effectiveness" flavored rhetoric about mathematics mirroring cosmic order should point to this claim for the E1 content and to the Platonism claim for the (separate, E4) interpretation.

**Red-team notes:** Both sources verified. The Wigner essay is correctly cited (Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics 13(1), 1-14, 1960, based on the 1959 Courant Lecture at NYU) and the PDF mirror is a standard, widely used copy of the original text. The PhilPapers entry correctly indexes the piece. Tier E1 is the right call for the framing used here — "E1 as an observation" is a defensible, narrow reading: the historical record of specific mathematical tools (complex numbers in QM, Riemannian geometry in GR, group representation theory in particle physics) finding unanticipated physical application is itself well documented and not contested as history. However, this is a softer E1 than a proven theorem or a replicated experiment — it is closer to a documented historiographical pattern than to E1's usual "experimentally confirmed, reproducible" standard, which is why confidence is correctly marked medium rather than high. The claim earns its keep specifically by NOT sliding into the Platonist reading (correctly deferred to a separate `mathematical-platonism` claim at E4) and by naming concrete rival explanations (selection bias, abstraction-from-experience, anthropic effects) rather than treating Platonism as the only live option — this is the correct calibration per CLAUDE.md's consciousness-specific caution analog for over-claiming.
