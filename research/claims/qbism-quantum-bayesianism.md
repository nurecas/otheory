steelman: "If quantum states encode an individual agent's subjective (though rationally constrained, via a Dutch-book-style consistency requirement and the Born rule as a normative addition to probability theory) degrees of belief about the outcomes of their own future measurement interventions, then 'collapse' is just an agent updating their beliefs upon getting new data — exactly as in ordinary Bayesian updating — and there is no physical action-at-a-distance or observer-caused physical collapse to explain. QBists argue this dissolves the measurement problem and the appearance of nonlocality without denying any experimental fact."
strongest_objection: "Critics (e.g., Norsen) argue QBism purchases its peace by giving up on quantum mechanics describing an observer-independent world at all, making it silent on what is actually happening rather than solving the problem — a strategy some regard as changing the subject rather than resolving the paradox. It also faces the challenge of explaining why different agents' subjective probability assignments so reliably intersubjectively agree (the 'inter-agent consistency' problem highlighted by Wigner's-friend-style scenarios), which requires additional argument (Frauchiger-Renner) to fully address."
falsifiability: "Not empirically distinguishable from other interpretations since it reproduces standard quantum predictions by construction; as an explicitly agent-centered epistemic stance about the meaning of probability, it is a philosophical position (comparable to subjective Bayesianism in general probability theory) rather than a further physical hypothesis, so it is properly E4, not E2."

RED-TEAM NOTES (2026-07-07): Both sources verified. Healey's SEP entry (author confirmed via WebFetch: Richard
Healey, University of Arizona, last revised Feb 2022/2023) is the standard secondary reference and correctly
summarized — QBism rejects objective/ontic quantum states in favor of personal Bayesian credence, exactly as
stated. Fuchs, Mermin & Schack (2014), American Journal of Physics 82, 749 (arXiv:1311.5253), is the canonical
QBism primer by its principal proponents, correctly cited and represented.

Tier: CONFIRMED E4, per CLAUDE.md's explicit instruction that QBism (as a purely epistemic/philosophical stance)
leans E4 rather than E2, unlike physically-motivated interpretations such as relational QM or consistent histories.
This claim already reasons through the distinction correctly in its own falsifiability field: QBism is "a
philosophical position... rather than a further physical hypothesis." This is the right call — QBism makes no new
physical claim distinguishable from orthodox QM even in principle, and its central content is a stance on what
probability and the quantum state ARE (an epistemological/metaphysical question about the semantics of quantum
states), which is precisely the E4 category (philosophical, not empirically decidable even in principle).

The closing note correctly heads off the most tempting misuse: QBism's "the state is in the agent's mind" framing
can look superficially like support for "consciousness is fundamental" physics, but QBists (Fuchs et al.
explicitly) intend this as a claim about any rational agent's belief-management using a normative extension of
Bayesian probability theory, not a claim that consciousness generates or substrates physical reality. This
distinction is essential and correctly enforced. No changes to tier or text needed beyond confirming status.
