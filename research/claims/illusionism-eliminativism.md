steelman: "Illusionists argue the 'hard problem' is generated by a cognitive illusion, not a real explanatory gap: our introspective mechanisms represent experiences as having simple, ineffable, intrinsic qualitative properties (qualia) that outstrip any functional description, but this representation is itself just another functional/representational process that could in principle be fully explained by cognitive science. On this view, once the 'illusion problem' (why and how we come to judge that experience is ineffable) is solved, nothing further — no residual phenomenality — is left over to explain, dissolving rather than solving the hard problem, and this is defended as the more parsimonious, naturalistically tractable option. Frankish's paper appeared as the lead target article of a JCS special issue with replies from prominent defenders including Dennett, Humphrey, Pereboom, and Rey, indicating the position is taken seriously by leading naturalist philosophers of mind, not merely a fringe view."
strongest_objection: "Critics (including many physicalists) call illusionism self-undermining or the 'strongest of the weak' worry: the very existence of an introspective appearance of phenomenality is itself a phenomenal fact (there being something it is like to have that illusion), so denying phenomenal consciousness while granting a phenomenal-seeming illusion appears to concede the point at one remove rather than eliminate it. Chalmers frames this as the meta-problem's central puzzle: illusionism must explain the appearance without covertly relying on the reality it denies."
falsifiability: "Not empirically decidable even in principle: whether introspective reports track a real phenomenal property or a merely functional 'quasi-phenomenal' one is a question about the correct interpretation of introspection, not a further fact any experiment could adjudicate beyond the functional data both sides already accept. Argued by reason; E4."

Illusionism is the most direct rival to every claim in this domain file that treats phenomenal consciousness as real and fundamental, including O Theory's core commitment: if illusionism is right, there is no phenomenal fact for consciousness to be fundamental about in the first place, so the entire dual-aspect/idealist family of positions (property dualism, panpsychism, Russellian monism, dual-aspect monism, analytic idealism) rests on a mistaken premise. It must be represented at full strength, not straw-manned, precisely because it is the sharpest philosophical challenge to the framework's founding assumption, and because it commands serious support among naturalist philosophers of mind (Frankish, Dennett, Humphrey, Rey), not just a token dissent. E4 throughout — an interpretive/conceptual dispute, not an empirical disagreement about brain data.
