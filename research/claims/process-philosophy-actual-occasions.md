steelman: "Substance metaphysics struggles to explain change and relation (how can an unchanging substance genuinely become different, and how are distinct substances really related rather than merely juxtaposed?); Whitehead's 'actual occasions' — momentary events of experiential 'concrescence' that internally incorporate ('prehend') prior occasions before perishing into data for future occasions — replace static substances with intrinsically relational, experiential process as the basic unit, offering a unified account of causal relatedness, temporal becoming, and (Whitehead argues) even a form of proto-experience at every level of nature (a view later commentators, e.g. process-theology and Griffin's school, label panexperientialism)."
strongest_objection: "Process philosophy purchases its relational and dynamic virtues at the cost of an enormously revisionary and technically opaque metaphysical apparatus (concrescence, prehension, eternal objects, God as 'principle of concretion') that is difficult to state precisely, difficult to connect to physical science's actual variables, and has never achieved more than a minority following even within metaphysics — critics argue it under-determines which entities in nature (an electron? a rock? a person?) actually qualify as unified 'actual occasions' versus mere aggregates."

Process philosophy holds that becoming, not static substance, is metaphysically basic: the world is composed of momentary "actual occasions" of experience that arise, briefly unify prior data, and perish into the past to be inherited by later occasions. This is the single most directly relevant academic precedent for O Theory's "process/experience as fundamental" framing, and should be cited as such rather than reinvented — Whitehead explicitly builds panexperientialism (proto-experience at every level, not just in brains) into his basic ontology. It remains E4: a systematic speculative metaphysics defended by philosophical argument and coherence with (1920s) physics, not a scientific theory with empirical predictions distinguishing it from substance-based alternatives.
