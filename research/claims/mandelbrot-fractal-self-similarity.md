steelman: "A fractal is a set exhibiting self-similarity across scales and a Hausdorff (fractal) dimension that can exceed its topological dimension — a precise, well-defined mathematical concept (e.g. the Mandelbrot set z→z²+c, the Koch curve, Cantor set). Mandelbrot showed such objects are not pathological curiosities but good quantitative models for coastlines, river networks, lung and vascular branching, and turbulence, giving measurable, testable fractal dimensions rather than vague resemblance."
strongest_objection: "The mathematics of fractals (dimension, iterated function systems, the Mandelbrot set's properties) is proven and precise — E1. Claims that specific natural systems are 'fractal,' however, are empirical and a matter of degree: most natural 'fractals' hold only over a finite range of scales (unlike true mathematical fractals, which are self-similar at all scales down to a point), and asserting a literal, unlimited, or metaphysically necessary 'scale-free recursion' throughout nature or the cosmos overstates what the finite-range empirical fits support."
falsifiability: "The pure mathematics is proven, not falsifiable. Fractal geometry is settled mathematics (E1: the definitions of self-similarity and fractal dimension, and specific objects like the Mandelbrot set, are proven and precise); its application to specific natural systems is a separate, empirical claim (E1/E2 depending on the system) that holds only over finite scale ranges. This is directly relevant to O Theory's invocation of "scale-free/fractal recursion" as a cosmic organizing principle: the real mathematics supports finite-range self-similarity in many physical systems, but an unbounded, literally scale-free recursion "all the way up and down" the cosmos is a speculative extrapolation beyond what any measured fractal dimension establishes, and should be labeled as analogy or speculation, not as the same claim as Mandelbrot's mathematics.
