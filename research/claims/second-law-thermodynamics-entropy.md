steelman: "The second law is among the most empirically robust generalizations in physics: in every isolated macroscopic system observed, entropy (a measure of the number of microstates compatible with a macrostate, S = k_B ln W) increases or stays constant, never decreases. It underlies the direction of heat flow, the impossibility of perpetual motion machines of the second kind, and the thermodynamic arrow of time. Lieb & Yngvason (1999) gave a rigorous axiomatic derivation of entropy and the second law from a small set of physically motivated postulates (a 'comparison principle' of adiabatic accessibility between equilibrium states), putting the law on solid mathematical footing within statistical mechanics."
strongest_objection: "The law is statistical, not absolute: for small systems or short times, entropy can fluctuate downward (fluctuation theorems), and the law's ultimate justification — why the universe began in a low-entropy state (the 'past hypothesis') — remains a genuinely open problem in the foundations of statistical mechanics and cosmology. Henriksson (2019) further notes a philosophical tension in subjectivist (Bayesian/epistemic) interpretations of entropy: if entropy increase reflects our changing knowledge rather than an objective feature of nature, it sits awkwardly with treating the arrow of time as a fundamental, mind-independent property. So E1 applies to the law as observed and derived for macroscopic isolated systems; deeper questions about the universe's initial conditions and the interpretation of probability in entropy remain open and are sometimes conflated, inappropriately, with metaphysical claims about time or consciousness."
falsifiability: "Falsified by any reproducible observation of a macroscopic isolated system's entropy spontaneously and persistently decreasing beyond statistical fluctuation. No such observation has ever been made across more than a century of thermodynamic and statistical-mechanical experiments; the law is continuously corroborated in engineering and physics contexts today."

The second law is E1: an established, statistically derived regularity with a rigorous axiomatic foundation (Lieb-Yngvason) and unbroken empirical support. It matters for O Theory chiefly through its later entanglement with information theory and black-hole thermodynamics (see black-hole-entropy-area-law and wheeler-it-from-bit-information-physics), which some interpret as suggesting entropy/information is more fundamental than matter — an extrapolation, not something the second law itself establishes.
