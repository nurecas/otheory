steelman: "Bell (1964) proved that any theory obeying 'local realism' (measurement outcomes are predetermined and no influence propagates faster than light) must satisfy a statistical inequality on correlations between distant measurements. Clauser (1972), Aspect (1980s), and Zeilinger's group, culminating in loophole-free experiments (Hensen et al. 2015 and contemporaneous NIST/Vienna results), measured correlations that violate this inequality exactly as quantum mechanics predicts, closing the detection and locality loopholes. This is a clean, repeatedly replicated experimental result and the basis of the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics — E1 at the observational level."
strongest_objection: "'Nonlocality' is a precise technical term here: it means quantum correlations cannot be reproduced by any theory in which distant measurement settings only causally influence local outcomes via signals at or below light speed, given also 'measurement independence' (free choice of settings) and 'outcome realism' style assumptions. It does NOT license faster-than-light signaling (the no-signaling theorem is itself a proven consequence of QM), nor does it establish that consciousness, observation, or any mental act is doing the correlating — the correlations exist and are confirmed regardless of whether anyone looks at the data (they can be extracted from recorded, uninspected files). Superdeterminism and retrocausal theories remain logically available (if unpopular) escapes from 'nonlocality' language, since they reject 'measurement independence' rather than locality."
falsifiability: "Already tested exhaustively: the correlations either violate the CHSH/Bell inequality bound of 2 (classical) up to quantum mechanics' predicted ~2.83 or they do not; loophole-free experiments confirm violation at high statistical significance. A future experiment finding no violation under matched conditions would falsify this, but none has."
