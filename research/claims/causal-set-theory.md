steelman: "Sorkin's causal set hypothesis takes two ideas seriously as fundamental: (1) spacetime is discrete at the Planck scale (finite volume regions contain only finitely many fundamental elements) and (2) the causal order between events is the primary structure, with metric/geometric properties emerging statistically in the continuum limit. This directly addresses black-hole entropy (a natural UV cutoff from discreteness) and, notably, led Sorkin (published version of a 1990 talk, Sorkin 1991) to predict a small positive cosmological constant of the right order of magnitude (~10^-120 in natural units) years before its 1998 observational discovery via distant-supernovae data — cited by proponents as a rare genuine successful prediction from a quantum-gravity research program, though the prediction was an order-of-magnitude estimate rather than a precise calculation."
strongest_objection: "Causal set theory has not yet produced a full, agreed-upon dynamics (a 'quantum measure' or path-integral sum over causal sets) that recovers classical general relativity in the appropriate limit for realistic spacetimes; it remains one of the smaller, less-resourced quantum-gravity programs compared to string theory or LQG, and its cosmological-constant 'prediction' is disputed as to how genuinely predictive versus retrodictive/order-of-magnitude it was."
falsifiability: "In principle falsifiable via discreteness-induced Lorentz-invariance violations (constrained but not ruled out by high-energy cosmic-ray and gamma-ray observations) or via detailed cosmological signatures of a discrete early universe; current tests are consistency checks rather than confirmations, and Planck-scale discreteness signatures remain beyond direct experimental reach today."

Causal set theory is E2: a minority but mathematically serious quantum-gravity program, notable for one retrospectively striking (if contested) successful order-of-magnitude prediction of the cosmological constant, but without a completed dynamics or direct experimental confirmation. It is included in the domain survey to round out the "spacetime might be discrete" family of ideas alongside LQG; both share a family resemblance ("discreteness at the Planck scale") that is a genuine mathematical proposal, not evidence for any information-theoretic or consciousness-related interpretation of that discreteness.
