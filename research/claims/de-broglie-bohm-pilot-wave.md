steelman: "Particles always have definite positions and follow deterministic trajectories set by a 'guiding equation' derived from the universal wavefunction, which itself always evolves unitarily (no collapse postulate needed). Given the standard 'quantum equilibrium' distribution of initial particle positions, the theory reproduces every statistical prediction of orthodox quantum mechanics, including Bell-inequality violation (via explicit nonlocality in the guiding equation) and the double-slit pattern, while restoring a single, observer-independent, deterministic ontology — solving the measurement problem by denying that the wavefunction alone is a complete description (claim 1 of the triad)."
strongest_objection: "It is empirically indistinguishable from standard quantum mechanics and other interpretations under the quantum equilibrium hypothesis, so no experiment currently favors it over, e.g., many-worlds or GRW collapse. It is also explicitly and irreducibly nonlocal (the guiding equation for one particle depends instantaneously on the positions of distant entangled particles), which some regard as a cost rather than a virtue, and extending it cleanly to relativistic quantum field theory remains an unresolved technical difficulty."
falsifiability: "Under the quantum equilibrium hypothesis it is not falsifiable against orthodox QM (identical predictions); in principle, deviations from quantum equilibrium ('quantum non-equilibrium') would produce observable departures from Born-rule statistics, and some cosmological/early-universe scenarios have been proposed to search for relic non-equilibrium effects, but no such effect has been detected."

RED-TEAM NOTES (2026-07-07): All three sources verified. Goldstein's SEP entry on Bohmian mechanics is the standard
reference, accurately summarized (guiding equation, quantum equilibrium hypothesis, reproduction of orthodox
statistics). Bohm (1952), Physical Review 85, 166-193, is the correct original two-part paper. Duerr, Goldstein &
Zanghi (2013), Quantum Physics Without Quantum Philosophy, Springer, is a real, well-known monograph by leading
Bohmian-mechanics researchers, correctly cited.

Tier confirmed E2 — a fully worked-out, mathematically rigorous alternative dynamics with a real (if minority)
research community, empirically equivalent to orthodox QM under quantum equilibrium and therefore not
experimentally favored over rivals. The falsifiability field is honest and appropriately nuanced: identical
predictions under equilibrium, but a genuine (if currently untested) route to falsifiability via quantum
non-equilibrium effects — this is accurate to the actual research literature (e.g. Valentini's work on relic
non-equilibrium), not an invented escape hatch.

The closing note's point is well-taken and important for this project: Bohmian mechanics achieves full determinism
and explicit nonlocality with zero role for consciousness or observation as a physical cause — nonlocality lives
in the particle dynamics (the guiding equation), not in anyone's mind looking at anything. This is a legitimate
and useful counterexample to any narrative that treats "quantum weirdness implies mind is doing something
physical." No changes needed beyond confirming status.
