steelman: "Bem's original paper used standard, well-validated cognitive-psychology paradigms (e.g., priming, recall) run in reverse temporal order and reported small but statistically significant effects in 9 of 10 experiments (combined N in the thousands), published after peer review in a mainstream APA journal. A later meta-analysis by Bem and colleagues (2015) claimed the effect held up across 90 replication attempts by various labs (overall small positive effect, p < .001), which proponents cite as evidence the phenomenon is a real, if small, statistical regularity."
strongest_objection: "Three independent, preregistered, direct replication attempts of the specific 'retroactive facilitation of recall' experiment (Edinburgh, Goldsmiths, Hertfordshire; combined N=150, 50 per site) all failed to find a significant effect — confirmed exact result: combined one-tailed p = .83, essentially the null hypothesis's own expectation — and the Journal of Personality and Social Psychology's own editorial process was criticized for using flexible statistical practices later identified as classic p-hacking risk factors (undisclosed optional stopping, multiple comparisons, post-hoc exclusions); Bem's paper is frequently cited in the psychology replication crisis literature as a paradigm case motivating preregistration reforms (e.g., it is a standard example in discussions following the 2011-2015 replication crisis). The 2015 pro-psi meta-analysis has itself been criticized for including a heterogeneous mix of studies with publication bias and researcher-degrees-of-freedom concerns, and its 90-study base includes many small, non-preregistered studies from parapsychology-friendly labs — it is not an independent disconfirmation of the Ritchie/Wiseman/French null, since it aggregates a different and much more heterogeneous study set rather than replicating the specific null result away. No mechanism consistent with known physics has been proposed for information to travel backward in time to influence prior neural states, and doing so would contradict well-established causal/thermodynamic asymmetries. Given a well-conducted direct replication program returning null results and no plausible physical mechanism, this is classified E6 (presents as an empirical claim but fails vetting) and marked rejected as a claim, not a live E3 minority science position."
falsifiability: "Yes — and it has been tested: direct preregistered replications are exactly the right test and were run, returning null results. The claim remains falsifiable in principle (further large preregistered replications could in principle detect an effect), but the existing replication record is strongly negative, which is why this is tiered E6 rather than an open E3 question."

An empirical claim about a specific, well-documented episode: a published positive result followed by a well-conducted null replication program. Tiered E6 (not E3) because, unlike orch-or-microtubules or other heterodox-but-live minority positions, the direct, preregistered replication attempts specifically targeting this effect returned null results and no viable physical mechanism has been proposed — this is closer to "failed to replicate and unsupported by any known mechanism" than to "contested but still actively defended by a credentialed minority using accepted methods." Relevant to any O Theory claim invoking psi/precognition as evidence for non-local mind: the replication record here should discourage citing Bem's original result as support.
