steelman: "The Pali canon and later exegetical (Abhidhamma/Visuddhimagga) tradition describe a highly structured, reproducible sequence of meditative absorptions (four Buddhist jhānas, or Vedāntic/Yogic samādhi stages), each marked by specific reported changes — from the suppression of the five hindrances and appearance of a 'counterpart sign,' through waning of directed thought and sustained thought, to increasingly refined equanimity and, in the formless jhānas, dissolution of spatial/conceptual content. This is a matured first-person contemplative technology with a highly consistent internal map, refined by practitioners over centuries and cross-checked within a living training lineage (teacher-verified reports), which is unusual rigor for introspective report."
strongest_objection: "The 'reproducibility' is intra-traditional: practitioners are trained on the same conceptual map (the Visuddhimagga's stages) before they meditate, so convergent reports may partly reflect shared instruction and expectation rather than a culture-independent natural kind — the same objection Katz raises against Stace's cross-tradition 'common core.' There is also no settled operational, third-person marker of 'jhāna attainment'; self-report and teacher assessment remain the only checks, and cross-lab neuroscience correlates of specific jhāna stages are preliminary and small-N (see meditation-dmn-neuroscience for the general meditation-neuroscience literature, which does not specifically validate jhāna stage-transitions). Teacher-verified report is also not independent verification in the third-person sense: teachers assess students against the same doctrinal map students were trained on, so agreement confirms shared training more than it confirms a mind-independent stage sequence."
falsifiability: "Contemplative/phenomenological report, not an empirical claim. The graded sequence is a first-person and lineage-transmitted taxonomy; it is not, on its own, a testable third-person hypothesis (though specific correlate-claims derived from it could be, and some are beginning to be studied — see the neuroscience-of-meditation literature for the general, not jhāna-specific, empirical program)."

This is a first-person CONTEMPLATIVE REPORT claim (E5): jhāna/samādhi describes a trained, graded sequence of absorption states with internally consistent phenomenological markers, reported within Buddhist (and cognate Hindu/Yogic) practice lineages. It is kept separate from any empirical measurement of these states (which is thin and preliminary, unlike the psilocybin/MEQ or general-meditation-DMN literatures) and from any metaphysical reading of what absorption "reveals." In O Theory's framing, the graded jhāna sequence is offered as a trainable, reproducible route to a maximally narrowed/quieted ordinary cognition — a candidate practice-based method of "widening the valve" — without that practice-efficacy implying anything about an O-Source's existence.
