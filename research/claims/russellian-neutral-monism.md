steelman: "Russell observed that physics describes matter only via its extrinsic, structural/relational properties (mass, charge, spin as measured by their effects) and never characterizes its categorical, intrinsic nature ('All that physics gives us is certain equations giving abstract properties of their changes. But as to what it is that changes... physics is silent,' Russell 1959: 18, quoted in SEP). Russellian monism argues consciousness gives us direct acquaintance with at least one instance of an intrinsic nature (a 'quiddity'), so it is economical (an application of Occam's razor, per Russell) to suppose the hidden intrinsic natures underlying physical structure just are, or are continuous with, the categorical basis of experience — unifying mind and matter without either reducing one to the other or positing two substances."
strongest_objection: "Critics press a trilemma of 'suspicions' documented in SEP's neutral-monism entry (secs. 4.2-4.3): the mentalism suspicion (the posited 'neutral' entities, e.g. Russell's percepts, look like disguised mental items, collapsing the view into idealism); the materialism suspicion (contemporary readings, e.g. Landini and Banks, treat the neutral entities as ultimately physical, collapsing it into physicalism instead); and, specifically for panprotopsychist Russellian monism, the combination problem (SEP's Russellian Monism entry calls it 'unsolved and serious') plus an emergence-based objection — if consciousness merely 'emerges' from protophenomenal properties via contingent causal laws rather than a priori entailment, the view loses its purported advantage over ordinary dualism."
falsifiability: "Not empirically decidable even in principle: physics can further characterize structural/relational properties but cannot, by its own methods, disclose or rule out an intrinsic categorical nature, so no experiment discriminates neutral monism from its physicalist or idealist neighbors. Argued by reason; E4."

Russellian monism is the specific academic lineage (Russell, and in its panprotopsychist form, Chalmers and Strawson) most structurally similar to a "dual-aspect" picture, since it denies either mind or matter is more fundamental and instead posits a common ground. It differs from dual-aspect monism proper by denying the ground itself has two aspects — it is neutral (a single kind of intrinsic nature that grounds both mental and physical descriptions), not dual (two co-fundamental aspects) — a distinction SEP's neutral-monism entry marks explicitly in its comparison section (4.6), while noting which relationship best describes the various historical positions "is still out" for debate. This claim now serves as the single canonical entry for both neutral monism proper (Russell's original view, and its mentalism/materialism-suspicion objections) and Russellian monism in its contemporary panprotopsychist form (Chalmers, Strawson), consolidating what were previously two adjacent claims. This is directly relevant background for evaluating whether O Theory's dual-aspect monism is a genuinely distinct position or a terminological variant of Russellian monism; both remain E4 arguments.
