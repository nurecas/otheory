steelman: "Many plants arrange leaves, florets, or seeds at successive divergence angles close to the golden angle (~137.508°), producing spiral counts (parastichies) that are consecutive Fibonacci numbers. Douady & Couder (1992) showed with physical experiments (magnetized droplets added sequentially at a growing center) and a dynamical model that this angle emerges as an attractor of a simple repulsion-minimization process at the growing tip (the meristem), not by design or an imposed aesthetic rule; Okabe (2015) further models it as minimizing the biomechanical energy cost of twisting the stem. This is a real, quantitatively confirmed, mechanistically explained empirical pattern."
strongest_objection: "The pattern is common but not universal — many plants show non-Fibonacci or irregular phyllotaxis, and Zhu et al. (2023, Quantitative Plant Biology) show that Fibonacci spiral counts can arise from local biochemical/mechanical interactions among primordia (e.g. auxin dynamics in gerbera) without the divergence angle being pinned to the exact golden angle, suggesting the golden angle is a robust attractor of the growth dynamics in many but not all cases, not a strict universal law of biology. This is entirely separate from, and should not be confused with, the much weaker and largely unsupported claim that φ governs aesthetics, art, or the human body (see `golden-ratio-ubiquity`, E6)."
falsifiability: "Empirical and already substantially tested: divergence angles are measurable in real plants and reproduced in physical/computational models; the mechanism (diffusion-limited or biochemical repulsion between successive primordia) makes falsifiable predictions about how divergence angle depends on the timing and geometry of primordium initiation, which have been checked experimentally and partially revised by subsequent work (Zhu et al. 2023)."
