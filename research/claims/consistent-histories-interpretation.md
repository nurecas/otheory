steelman: "Instead of privileging 'measurement' as a special physical process, this approach assigns probabilities to entire time-ordered sequences of coarse-grained properties ('histories'), but only when the candidate histories satisfy a 'consistency' (decoherence) condition ensuring quantum interference between them is negligible — Copenhagen-style probabilistic reasoning applied to closed systems including the whole universe, with no external observer needed, motivated explicitly by wanting a formalism usable in quantum cosmology where there is no outside experimenter."
strongest_objection: "The choice of which consistent family of histories to use is not fixed by the theory itself, and different, mutually incompatible consistent families can each be internally consistent while making contradictory-sounding statements (the same physical situation admits multiple valid 'consistent' narratives) — critics (e.g., Dowker & Kent) argue this underdetermination means the framework doesn't by itself explain why our world exhibits the single quasiclassical history we observe rather than some other equally consistent one."
falsifiability: "Reproduces standard quantum statistics for any given consistent family and is not distinguished experimentally from other no-collapse interpretations; the open technical question is internal (which family, and why quasiclassical domains are favored) rather than an experimental one."

RED-TEAM NOTES (2026-07-07): All three sources verified. Griffiths (1984), Journal of Statistical Physics 36,
219-272, is the correct founding paper. Gell-Mann & Hartle (1990), the "Quantum mechanics in the light of quantum
cosmology" chapter in Zurek's edited volume, is correctly cited (mirrored on arXiv). Griffiths' own SEP entry
confirmed via WebFetch (substantively revised June 2024) and directly corroborates the claim's core content: "the
concept of measurement... no longer plays a fundamental role. Instead, all quantum time dependence is
probabilistic," and "measurements are treated in the same way as all other physical processes and play no special
role in the interpretation."

One nuance worth recording: the SEP entry itself, being written from a broadly proponent (Griffiths) perspective,
frames the multiple-incompatible-families issue more as a pluralist feature ("No framework is more fundamental
than any other") than as an unresolved problem, whereas the claim's strongest_objection cites Dowker & Kent's
actual published critique that this underdetermination is a genuine explanatory gap (why does OUR quasiclassical
history get realized/experienced, if many equally consistent families exist). This is not a misrepresentation —
Dowker & Kent's critique is real, published, and correctly attributed as a critics'-side objection, appropriately
distinguished from the proponents' own framing. This is exactly the kind of "steelman vs. strongest objection"
balance the vetting process should produce, and it is handled correctly here.

Tier confirmed E2 — mathematically developed by working physicists including a Nobel laureate (Gell-Mann, 1969
Physics Nobel for unrelated particle-physics work, correctly not conflated with this framework's own standing),
taken seriously in quantum cosmology, not experimentally distinguished from rivals. Falsifiability field is honest.
The closing note's point stands: like RQM and QBism, this framework removes rather than elevates any special
observer role, directly counter to consciousness-collapse narratives. No changes needed beyond confirming status.
