steelman: "Historians of science (Newman, Principe, Dobbs) have shown alchemy was substantially real laboratory practice — distillation, sublimation, mineral acid preparation, metallurgy — continuous with what became chemistry, alongside a rich symbolic/allegorical vocabulary (the four elements, the Great Work, the Emerald Tablet) used to describe both material processes and spiritual transformation. Isaac Newton's extensive alchemical manuscripts, studied seriously by Dobbs, show a working experimentalist, not merely a mystic."
strongest_objection: "The specific goal most associated with alchemy in popular imagination — the literal transmutation of base metals into gold via the 'philosopher's stone,' and the elixir of life granting immortality — was never achieved and is inconsistent with the modern understanding of elements as defined by atomic number (transmutation between elements requires nuclear, not chemical, processes). The symbolic/spiritual register (inner transformation, the 'Great Work' as psychological or soteriological allegory, as later read by Jung) is a separate interpretive claim from the historical chemistry and should not be conflated with it. Symbol/practice and real proto-chemical history, not a validated transmutation technology."
falsifiability: "The proto-chemical practices are historically documented and their real chemical results (compounds produced, techniques developed) are checkable against the historical/chemical record — this part is settled history, not in dispute. The transmutation-of-metals goal was empirically tested over centuries and never achieved by chemical means; it is falsified as chemistry (modern nuclear physics explains why: elemental identity is fixed by atomic number, unreachable by chemical reaction). The symbolic/spiritual reading is E5, not an empirical claim."

Alchemy is split cleanly: as documented laboratory practice and symbolic/spiritual allegory it is E5 (real history, real symbol system, honestly attributed to the "chymistry" tradition per Newman and Principe's scholarship); as a claim to have achieved or to reliably achieve metallic transmutation or immortality it was tested for centuries and failed, and would be E6 if asserted today as working chemistry. Relevant to O Theory wherever alchemical or "transformation" symbolism is used — it should be framed as historical/symbolic practice, not as a suppressed or validated physical technology.
