"It from bit" is Wheeler's late-career thesis that the physical world is, at bottom, information-theoretic: particles, fields, and spacetime derive their existence from binary, apparatus-elicited answers to yes-no questions, in a "participatory universe." This is the closest well-known physicist-authored precedent for O Theory's "information as substrate" language, but it is philosophy of physics argued from an interpretive reading of quantum measurement, not an experimentally confirmed result — hence E4, not E1/E2. Distinguish carefully from the observation that quantum information theory is mathematically fruitful (well established, E1/E2) versus the interpretation that information is thereby the ultimate stuff of reality (E4, contested).
