steelman: "Kastrup argues that mainstream physicalism cannot bridge the explanatory gap (the hard problem) and that dualism faces the interaction problem, whereas idealism needs only one ontological primitive, consciousness, of which we already have direct acquaintance (unlike matter, known only through its effects). He models individual minds as dissociated 'alters' of one universal consciousness — analogous to dissociative identity disorder — arguing this explains why private, bounded experience arises within a single mental substrate without positing a second, non-mental kind of stuff. Kastrup states explicitly that this ontology 'does not fall prey to' the hard problem of consciousness (physicalism's difficulty), the combination problem (bottom-up panpsychism's difficulty), or the decombination problem (cosmopsychism's difficulty) in turn, positioning it as claiming a comparative advantage over all three rival families rather than merely restating one of them."
strongest_objection: "Analytic idealism remains a minority position within professional philosophy of mind, and critics argue the dissociation analogy does not resolve but merely relocates the decombination problem: DID alters are themselves phenomena occurring within a single already-embodied physical brain, so borrowing the concept to explain how one undifferentiated cosmic consciousness produces many bounded subjects risks smuggling in the very brain-based individuation it is meant to avoid explaining metaphysically. Mainstream physicalists and panpsychists further object that idealism must explain the intersubjectively stable, quantitatively precise regularities of physics as mere appearances within a single mind, without a principled account of why they take exactly the mathematical form they do."
falsifiability: "Not empirically decidable even in principle — idealism and physicalism are compatible with the same observed regularities, differing only on which side (mind or matter) is ontologically fundamental. A minority academic metaphysical position argued by reason; E4."

Kastrup holds two PhDs (philosophy and computer engineering) and publishes in peer-reviewed venues (Journal of Consciousness Studies, Erkenntnis, Disputatio), which distinguishes analytic idealism from purely popular mysticism, but it remains a minority position relative to physicalism, panpsychism, and cosmopsychism within academic philosophy of mind. It is the closest existing named academic position to O Theory's "consciousness is fundamental, individual minds are localized/dissociated expressions of one field" framing, and must be presented as a serious but contested minority argument (E4), with its objections given full weight, never as an established or majority view. Physicalism/functionalism, not idealism, remains the field's default majority position — this claim must not be read, in isolation or in synthesis, as suggesting otherwise.
