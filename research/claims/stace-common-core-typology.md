steelman: "Stace catalogued recurring first-person descriptors across many contemplative traditions — unity, non-spatiotemporality (introvertive) or a sense of all things interpenetrating as 'alive' (extrovertive), a noetic quality (felt as revelatory), positive affect, sacredness, paradoxicality, and ineffability. As a phenomenological taxonomy built from reports, it organizes an otherwise scattered first-person literature and gave later researchers (Hood) a structure to operationalize."
strongest_objection: "Stace's typology is itself an interpretive abstraction over reports selected and translated across traditions, so its 'commonality' may reflect the categories Stace brought to the texts rather than a discovered cross-cultural invariant (this is precisely Katz's objection, filed separately as an E4 dispute). It is also not falsifiable as a report-classification scheme, only useful or not. A further, sharper risk for this project specifically: every downstream 'validated' psychometric instrument (MEQ30, Hood's M-Scale) is built by writing items directly from Stace's own categories, so their factor-analytic 'confirmation' of a common structure is close to circular — they confirm that Stace's categories cluster together in questionnaires seeded with Stace's categories, not that the categories are an independently discovered natural kind. This circularity risk should be read into every empirical claim downstream of this one."
falsifiability: "Phenomenological/typological report, not an empirical claim in itself; it is a descriptive taxonomy of first-person accounts, not a third-person measurable hypothesis. Downstream psychometric instruments (e.g., Hood's M-Scale, MEQ30) attempt to operationalize it and are the empirically testable layer, though see the circularity caveat above."

This is a first-person-report-derived TYPOLOGY (E5, symbolic/contemplative), not an empirical or metaphysical claim: it describes the recurring phenomenological features mystics across traditions report (unity, ineffability, noetic quality, sacredness, paradoxicality), and Stace argued a common core underlies surface doctrinal differences. It is separate from (a) the empirical question of whether these features cluster statistically in questionnaire data (see meq30-mystical-experience-psychometrics, hood-mysticism-scale-psychometrics — noting the psychometric-circularity risk flagged above) and (b) the metaphysical question of whether the reported "unity" reveals a real non-dual ground (see perennialism-constructivism-debate, mystical-experience-epistemic-status). Relevant to O Theory only as a candidate first-person signature of a "widened valve," never as evidence the valve contacts anything real.
