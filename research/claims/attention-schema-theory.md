steelman: "AST offers a concrete, evolutionarily-motivated, computationally implementable mechanism: just as the brain builds a schematic body model to monitor and control movement, it builds a schematic 'attention model' to monitor and control its own selective-attention process, and claims of subjective awareness are the reports this model generates. It has been partially implemented in an artificial deep Q-learning neural-network agent (Wilterson & Graziano 2021, PNAS) that used an attention-schema module to control visuospatial attention more effectively than an equivalent agent without one, showing the mechanism is at least computationally coherent and useful for a control problem — a rare case of a consciousness theory yielding a working engineering demonstration."
strongest_objection: "The PNAS demonstration shows an attention-schema module improves an agent's attentional control; it does not show, and cannot show, that the agent thereby has subjective experience — the same gap between functional/computational success and the presence of phenomenal experience that afflicts every functionalist theory (the 'hard problem' objection applies directly). AST is explicitly allied with illusionism (Dennett, Frankish): it proposes that the FEELING of having qualia is itself just what an inaccurate self-model reports, which many philosophers regard as changing the subject (explaining away the explanandum) rather than explaining phenomenal consciousness. Because the theory's central claim — that a self-model's report of awareness IS the awareness, with no further fact about phenomenal experience — is a philosophical stance about the nature of experience rather than an empirically adjudicable mechanism, it is not testable in the way its computational implementation is; the implementation tests attention control, not sentience. Hence E4, philosophical, despite legitimate empirical/computational work motivating it."
falsifiability: "The narrow engineering claim (an attention-schema module improves an artificial agent's attentional control) is testable and was tested. The core philosophical claim (that this self-model constitutes, rather than merely accompanies or reports on, subjective experience) is not empirically decidable — no experiment distinguishes 'the schema constitutes awareness' from 'the schema is a functional model that happens to accompany awareness produced some other way,' so the theory of consciousness proper is classified E4."

Distinguishes the working computational/functional result (a self-model module aids attention control — real, demonstrated, uncontroversial as engineering) from the philosophical claim that this self-model IS subjective awareness (illusionism about phenomenal consciousness — an E4 stance in the same family as functionalism and higher-order theories generally). Relevant to O Theory's produced-vs-localized framing as a leading "consciousness is entirely brain-produced, and even the feeling of ineffable experience is itself just a model" position, i.e., the strongest available alternative to any non-local/filter account.
