The 2013 holographic-mass paper's stated proton charge radius sits close to the anomalously small muonic value canonized by the proton-radius puzzle, and Haramein correctly emphasizes that a proton's mass-energy should include vacuum/energy-density contributions the naive rest-mass ignores. The work is presented with equations, a falsifiable-sounding numerical target, and (unlike pure word-salad) an explicit physical picture (torus/holofractographic vacuum structure)."
strongest_objection: "It fails elementary vetting as physics on several independent counts. (1) Imposing the Schwarzschild condition on a proton makes it a black hole of mass ~10^14 g — about 38 orders of magnitude heavier than the measured proton mass (~1.673×10^-24 g); a single hydrogen nucleus would then weigh nearly a billion tonnes, which is flatly false (McElrath, via Thrive Debunked). (2) The derivation is circular: assuming a proton is a black hole and then 'finding' that two such objects attract with nuclear-strength forces is built into the premise, not derived. (3) The venue is non-refereed: AIP Conference Proceedings 1303 is the self-selected CASYS'09 conference volume, and the 'Best Paper Award' was a conference-section honor, not journal peer review; the later holographic-mass paper appeared in a SCIRP-family pay-to-publish outlet unrelated to the APS's Physical Review. (4) The celebrated 'proton radius prediction' is a post-diction: the small muonic-hydrogen radius (0.84184(67) fm) was already published by Pohl et al. in Nature in July 2010, over two years before Haramein's December 2012 copyright registration and 2013 paper. The framework has produced no confirmed novel prediction and is ignored, not engaged, by mainstream physics."
falsifiability: "The concrete physical consequence — a proton with Schwarzschild-scale mass (~10^14 g) — is already falsified by every mass measurement of the proton and of atomic nuclei (proton rest mass = 938.272 MeV/c^2 ≈ 1.673×10^-24 g). No unfalsified, genuinely novel, later-confirmed quantitative prediction exists; the proton-radius match is retrodictive (Pohl et al. 2010 preceded Haramein's claim). This is why the claim sits at E6 rather than as a live minority proposal."

Nassim Haramein (Resonance Science Foundation / International Space Federation; no located formal physics degree) advances a "unified/holofractographic physics" in which the proton obeys the Schwarzschild black-hole condition ("The Schwarzschild Proton," AIP Conf. Proc. 1303, pp. 95-100, 2010) and mass is a holographic count of Planck-scale voxels on a surface ("Quantum Gravity and the Holographic Mass," 2013). Presented explicitly as physics, it fails vetting on multiple independent grounds and is classified E6 / rejected. The Schwarzschild condition forces a proton mass of order 10^14 g — 38 orders of magnitude above the measured value and flatly contradicted by nuclear masses; Bob McElrath's widely cited analysis (summarized at Thrive Debunked, verified) further shows the reasoning is circular (a black-hole proton is assumed, then nuclear-strength forces are "discovered") and notes a single hydrogen nucleus would weigh nearly a billion tonnes. The 2010 venue is not a peer-reviewed physics journal: AIP Conference Proceedings 1303 is the non-refereed CASYS'09 volume (confirmed via ADS bibcode 2010AIPC.1303...95H; the "Best Paper" honor was a conference-section award), and the later holographic-mass paper appeared in a SCIRP pay-to-publish outlet distinct from the American Physical Society's Physical Review. The frequently repeated "proton-radius prediction" is retrodictive: the anomalously small muonic-hydrogen charge radius (0.84184(67) fm) was published by Pohl et al. in Nature 466:213-216 in July 2010 (verified), before Haramein's December 2012 registration, so the framework matched an already-known number rather than predicting an unknown one. Genuine, established components it borrows from (the Bekenstein-Hawking area law, real holography/AdS-CFT, general relativity) are tiered separately at E1-E2 and must not be used to launder this proposal's credibility.
