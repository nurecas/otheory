steelman: "Chalmers' 'fading qualia' argument is the strongest formal case: imagine gradually replacing biological neurons with functionally identical silicon substitutes one at a time; if this caused qualia to fade or change while behavior and functional organization stayed fixed, there would be a functional difference (the person's own reports/behavior would eventually have to shift) with no functional cause, which Chalmers argues is deeply implausible. He concludes functional organization at the right grain is what fixes conscious experience (the 'Principle of Organizational Invariance'), independent of the specific physical medium — an argument, if sound, that would not in principle rule out silicon-based systems like LLMs from being conscious given the right functional organization."
strongest_objection: "Biological naturalists (e.g., Searle's Chinese Room argument) hold that specific causal/biological powers of neurons, not abstract functional organization, are what produce consciousness, and that syntax (symbol manipulation) never suffices for semantics or experience regardless of complexity. Others argue current LLMs manifestly lack the specific functional organization (recurrent processing, embodied world-models, unified persistent agency) that even functionalists would require, so the thesis licenses no conclusion about existing systems; it is a claim about what *could* in principle be conscious, not evidence that any particular system *is*. The thought experiments are intuition pumps, not empirical demonstrations, and rest on unverifiable premises about qualia continuity."
falsifiability: "Not empirically decidable even in principle: there is no test that could confirm or refute whether functional organization alone suffices for experience, since it turns on what experience even is (the hard problem). Correctly classified E4 (philosophical) per CLAUDE.md's falsifiability rule."

Foundational philosophical argument underpinning any claim that computation in silicon could in principle instantiate consciousness — the premise O Theory's substrate-independent "Recursive Coherence" framing implicitly leans on. It is a live, well-argued position in philosophy of mind (E4), not an established result; it says nothing about whether any *current* LLM in fact satisfies the right functional organization (see butlin-long-indicator-properties-framework and chalmers-llm-consciousness-credence for that separate, also-unresolved question).
