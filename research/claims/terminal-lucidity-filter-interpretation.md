steelman: "If consciousness/memory were strictly identical to, and produced entirely by, a severely degraded neural substrate (with widespread cortical atrophy, plaque burden, and neuron loss), it is at least surprising that structured, personally-coherent recall and communication would transiently reappear; a filter/transmission model (mind is not generated but only expressed/constrained by the brain, and constraint can transiently relax) is one way to make sense of that surprise, and has philosophical proponents (e.g., William James's 'transmission' hypothesis, revived by some philosophers of mind)."
strongest_objection: "The observation does not require this interpretation and is not evidence for it over ordinary neuroscience: dementia pathology is patchy and dynamic, not a uniform, monotonically-declining 'signal loss,' so transient local changes in inflammation, perfusion, neurotransmitter balance, or amyloid-oligomer state occurring in relatively spared circuits is a standard materialist explanation requiring no non-local mind, and it is the explanation active research programs (e.g., NIA-funded studies) are actually pursuing. The filter interpretation makes no distinct, testable prediction beyond the observation itself — it is unfalsifiable as stated, since any recovery of function is consistent with 'the filter loosened' and any absence of recovery is consistent with 'the filter stayed shut.' This is a metaphysical reading layered on an empirical phenomenon, not a competing scientific hypothesis; it belongs at E4, not at the tier of the observation it rides on."
falsifiability: "Not empirically decidable as stated — 'a filter loosened' and 'localized neural circuits transiently regained function' make the same observational predictions, so no experiment distinguishes them without additional, currently unspecified commitments (e.g., a mechanism by which non-local mind interacts with tissue). Classified E4, philosophical."
