steelman: "This was a randomized, double-blind, controlled trial: 30 hallucinogen-naive, spiritually-active adults received high-dose psilocybin (30 mg/70kg) versus an active comparator (methylphenidate) across counterbalanced sessions, with acute experience scored on Pahnke's Stace-derived mystical-experience criteria. A high dose reliably occasioned experiences meeting the criteria, and at 14-month follow-up (2008), the occurrence of a 'complete' mystical-type experience during the session statistically mediated durable, observer-corroborated increases in self-reported well-being, life satisfaction, and positive behavior change, with roughly one-third of participants rating the session among the top five (some, the single most) meaningful experiences of their lives. This design (blinding, active comparator, validated criteria, long follow-up, corroborating observer ratings) is unusually rigorous for a subjective-experience study."
strongest_objection: "Blinding is imperfect in psychedelic trials — participants and raters can often correctly guess who received the active drug from its unmistakable acute effects, which can inflate expectancy-driven positive ratings ('functional unblinding'); this is a well-documented, near-universal confound across the psychedelics-clinical-trial literature and is not fully resolved by the 2006/2008 design's active comparator, since methylphenidate's effects are also discriminable from psilocybin's. The sample (self-selected, spiritually engaged, hallucinogen-naive volunteers in a screened, supportive, hospital-like setting with trained guides who are themselves invested in a positive outcome) limits generalizability and introduces demand characteristics; 'mystical-type' explicitly flags that meeting Stace/Pahnke criteria is a descriptive/psychometric match, not a metaphysical judgment about veridicality. Long-term follow-up relies on retrospective self-report subject to memory reconstruction, and the same Stace-derived circularity risk noted for the MEQ30 applies to the Pahnke criteria used for acute scoring here."
falsifiability: "Empirical claim, falsifiable and largely replicated: the prediction (high-dose psilocybin under supportive conditions increases MEQ/mystical-criteria scores versus active control, and criterion-meeting sessions predict later well-being change) has been tested with blinding, dose-response designs, and long follow-ups in multiple subsequent Johns Hopkins and other-lab studies. What is NOT tested or claimed is whether the experience discloses any real metaphysical unity — that is a separate, non-empirical question."

This is an EMPIRICAL claim (E3: real, replicated drug effect on a validated measure, but with open expectancy/blinding confounds and generalizability limits): psilocybin, at sufficient dose under supportive conditions, reliably produces a self-rated "mystical-type" experience with measurably durable positive effects on well-being and attitudes. It says nothing, and Griffiths et al. explicitly do not claim anything, about whether the experience is metaphysically veridical — the "mystical-type" label is a descriptive match to Stace/Pahnke criteria, not an ontological verdict (kept separate in mystical-experience-epistemic-status). This is the strongest empirical anchor for O Theory's "chemically widened valve" framing, but the widening-produces-real-contact reading remains an unproven E4 interpretation layered on top of this E3 result.
