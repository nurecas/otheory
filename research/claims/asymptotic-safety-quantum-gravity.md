steelman: "Weinberg (1976) conjectured that gravity, though perturbatively non-renormalizable, could still be a well-defined quantum field theory if its renormalization-group flow approaches a non-trivial fixed point at high energy (asymptotic safety), bounding the number of free parameters at arbitrarily high energies. Using functional renormalization-group (FRG) methods pioneered by Reuter, numerous truncated calculations across the field robustly find evidence for such a fixed point ('Reuter fixed point') in gravity alone and with Standard Model matter added, without requiring new degrees of freedom (extra dimensions, strings, or discretized geometry) beyond ordinary quantum field theory and general relativity."
strongest_objection: "The FRG calculations rely on truncations (approximations that keep only a finite subset of the infinitely many possible terms in the effective action), and it is not proven that the fixed point survives in the untruncated, exact theory — Bonanno et al. (2020), a critical self-assessment by leading practitioners of the program, catalog open technical and conceptual questions and note the evidence, while suggestive and reproduced across many truncation schemes, falls short of a rigorous existence proof. The approach has made no confirmed experimental prediction distinguishing it from other quantum-gravity candidates."
falsifiability: "In principle falsifiable/confirmable via predictions for how gravitational couplings run with energy, with possible (currently speculative) signatures in trans-Planckian cosmology or black hole physics; at present no experiment operates near the Planck scale where distinguishing signatures would appear, so direct empirical tests are not currently feasible, though indirect consistency checks (e.g., with Higgs mass predictions in some asymptotic-safety-based cosmological scenarios) have been explored."
