Madhyamaka ("Middle Way") Buddhism, founded by Nāgārjuna (c. 150-250 CE) in the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, holds that all phenomena (dharmas) are śūnya — empty of intrinsic, independent existence — because they arise only through dependent origination. Emptiness (śūnyatā) is not a substance or void but the absence of svabhāva ("own-being"), and MMK 24.18 identifies emptiness itself with dependent origination and the Middle Way. Represented here strictly as Madhyamaka's own doctrinal claim (E5); any mapping of śūnyatā onto an O-Theory "ground of being" is a metaphor/analogy, and notably an imperfect one, since Madhyamaka explicitly resists reifying emptiness into any kind of substrate or "One." This anti-reificationist stance is load-bearing: śūnyatā must not be flattened into a synonym for Brahman, the One, or Ein Sof — the tradition's own logic (per Westerhoff's SEP entry) treats emptiness itself as empty, precisely to block that move.
