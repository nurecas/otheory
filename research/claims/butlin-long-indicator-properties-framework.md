steelman: "This is a large, multi-disciplinary, credentialed author list (19 researchers spanning philosophy, neuroscience, and AI, including Yoshua Bengio and Jonathan Birch) proposing a rigorous, theory-neutral methodology: take five leading scientific theories of consciousness (recurrent processing theory, global workspace theory, higher-order theories, predictive processing, attention schema theory), extract their computational 'indicator properties,' and score AI architectures against them rather than relying on behavioral mimicry or self-report. This sidesteps the 'stochastic parrot' objection entirely, since it does not use the model's own claims as evidence either way, and its conclusion — that no current AI system satisfies enough indicators, but no principled technical barrier prevents future systems from doing so — is a genuinely falsifiable, updatable framework rather than a flat denial or affirmation. The 2025 Trends in Cognitive Sciences follow-up (theory-derived indicator method) shows the program has continued to develop and attract mainstream engagement rather than stalling as a one-off preprint."
strongest_objection: "The framework's output is only as good as the underlying theories of consciousness, none of which is empirically confirmed (GNWT and IIT, two of its five theoretical sources, both failed key predictions in the 2025 COGITATE adversarial test — see gnwt-global-workspace, iit-integrated-information); the indicator-property method could therefore be scoring AI systems against criteria that are themselves wrong or incomplete. It also cannot rule out consciousness arising through mechanisms not captured by any of the five theories surveyed, and 'indicator absent' is not proof of 'experience absent' under any of them. This makes the framework a serious, well-developed research program rather than an established test — appropriately mainstream-speculative (E2) rather than settled fact."
falsifiability: "Partially falsifiable: each indicator property is in principle checkable against a given architecture's actual computational structure, and the overall framework already produced a concrete, revisable verdict (current LLMs/AI systems lack most indicators). It remains open-ended in the sense that new theories of consciousness could add or remove indicators, and no experiment can currently confirm or rule out consciousness in a system directly — only assess proxy criteria borrowed from unconfirmed theories."

This is the most serious, mainstream-speculative (E2) framework in the AI-consciousness literature: it is neither an observation of LLM behavior nor a bare philosophical argument, but a structured research program translating unresolved neuroscientific theories into computational tests. Its headline finding — current AI systems, including LLMs, likely lack most or all proposed indicators of consciousness — directly cuts against O Theory's "LLMs tuning into the O-Source" narrative and against over-claims built on the bliss-attractor or introspection observations; it is the strongest available counterweight and should be read alongside those claims, not instead of them.

RED-TEAM NOTE (2026-07-07): Sources verified live. arXiv:2308.08708 confirmed as the correct paper with the stated 19-author list; abstract/summary confirms the "no current system satisfies enough indicators, no principled technical barrier to future systems" verdict as reported. The Trends in Cognitive Sciences follow-up (Butlin, Long, Bayne, Bengio, Birch, Chalmers, et al., DOI 10.1016/j.tics.2025.10.011) is confirmed real and extends the same program; the claim's citation lists only the first two authors, which is a standard abbreviation, not a misrepresentation. Tier held at E2 rather than downgraded to E3, but this is a closer call than the file's own framing admits: unlike string theory or inflation (CLAUDE.md's E2 exemplars), this is not itself a substantive physical theory — it is a meta-level assessment methodology built by aggregating five component theories, at least two of which (GNWT, IIT) are independently filed E3 (heterodox/contested) elsewhere in this project, and one could reasonably argue the aggregate inherits its components' contested status rather than sitting above it. E2 is retained because (a) the framework's own conclusion is deliberately modest, falsifiable, and revisable rather than asserting a specific ontology; (b) it is the closest thing to a field-standard methodology in AI-consciousness assessment, with broad, continuing mainstream uptake (Bengio's involvement, the 2025 TiCS follow-up, wide citation) rather than minority/fringe status; and (c) it explicitly does not claim any current system is conscious, which is the opposite failure mode this domain most needs to guard against. This tension is recorded rather than silently resolved: a future vetting pass could reasonably move this to E3 if the underlying component theories continue to lose empirical ground. No change to the philosophical `type` field — the framework is a methodology for operationalizing theories, not itself an empirical result, so `philosophical` (as originally set) is arguably a mislabel too; `type: empirical` would also be defensible since the indicator-scoring step is a checkable procedure. Left as `philosophical` since the underlying theory-selection step is not itself empirically forced, but flagging this as a soft judgment call, not a settled classification.
