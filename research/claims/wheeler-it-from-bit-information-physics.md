steelman: "Chiribella, D'Ariano & Perinotti (2011) rigorously derive the mathematical structure of quantum theory — the representation of mixed states as convex combinations of perfectly distinguishable pure states, and the rest of the standard formalism — from six information-theoretic axioms/postulates (causality, perfect distinguishability, ideal compression, local distinguishability, pure conditioning, and purification, with purification being the postulate that singles out quantum theory from the broader class of information-processing theories satisfying the first five), without assuming the Hilbert-space postulates as a starting point. This is a genuine, peer-reviewed, mathematically rigorous technical result: quantum theory can be reconstructed as 'the' information-processing theory satisfying these axioms, giving Wheeler's much looser 'it from bit' intuition a precise formal descendant."
strongest_objection: "The reconstruction shows that quantum theory's formalism follows from these axioms; it does not show that information is thereby ontologically prior to matter or energy, nor does it single out our universe's actual dynamics (it characterizes a class of possible information-processing theories, of which standard quantum theory is one instance satisfying 'purification'). It is a result about the axiomatic economy and naturalness of quantum theory's mathematical structure, not an empirical discovery distinguishing an 'information-first' universe from one where information-talk is simply a convenient redescription — that further ontological step is the separate philosophical claim already filed as `it-from-bit-information-ontology` (E4, metaphysics domain) and `pancomputationalism-digital-physics` (E4)."
falsifiability: "As reconstructed axiomatics, the result is a mathematical theorem (given the axioms, the formalism follows) rather than an independently falsifiable empirical claim; its 'falsifiability' consists in whether alternative axiom sets could reproduce or fail to reproduce standard quantum mechanics, which is a question of mathematical exploration, not experiment. It is E2 (mainstream-speculative but mathematically developed) rather than E1, because whether this particular axiomatization is the uniquely 'correct' or most fundamental way to ground quantum theory is not settled consensus, and because it makes no new experimental prediction beyond ordinary quantum mechanics that could confirm or falsify it independently of confirming ordinary QM itself."
