steelman: "The Standard Model, built from Yang-Mills gauge symmetries SU(3)_color x SU(2)_weak x U(1)_hypercharge, unifies the electromagnetic, weak, and strong forces and the known matter fields into a single renormalizable quantum field theory. Its predictions (particle spectra, decay rates, coupling constants, and the Higgs boson itself, discovered at ~125-126 GeV in 2012 at 5-sigma-plus significance by two independent LHC detectors, ATLAS and CMS) have been confirmed to extraordinary precision over five decades, making it the most rigorously tested physical theory in existence."
strongest_objection: "The Standard Model is known to be incomplete: it does not include gravity, does not explain neutrino mass mechanisms in detail, has no dark-matter or dark-energy candidate, and contains ~19 free parameters with no explanation of their values (the 'hierarchy problem' and 'why these masses' questions remain open). So E1 applies to the SM as a confirmed effective field theory within its tested energy range, not as a final or complete theory of matter."
falsifiability: "Continuously tested at particle colliders (LHC) via precision measurements of particle masses, cross-sections, and rare decays; any statistically significant deviation from SM predictions (e.g., anomalous magnetic moments, unexpected new particles) would indicate new physics. This testing is ongoing and feasible with current and next-generation colliders."

Gauge symmetry is the deep organizing principle of the Standard Model, extending the same symmetry-to-conservation logic captured by Noether's theorem (see noether-symmetry-conservation) into the local (gauge) case that generates the forces themselves. This is E1: established, repeatedly confirmed, and precision-tested. It has no direct bearing on consciousness; where O Theory metaphors reach for "the quantum field" or "gauge symmetry" as an image of an underlying non-local substrate, that is an analogy/metaphor register, not a claim the Standard Model itself supports.

RED-TEAM NOTES (2026-07-07): Both ATLAS and CMS 2012 discovery papers independently verified by direct fetch: ATLAS paper confirmed reporting a boson at 126.0 +/- 0.4(stat) +/- 0.4(sys) GeV at 5.9-sigma significance, described as compatible with the SM Higgs. Provisional tier E1 confirmed correct — no stronger case exists in physics for E1 status than the SM's five-decade track record plus the Higgs discovery. No misrepresentation found; steelman and objection are accurate and appropriately scoped (E1 for the tested effective theory, explicitly not a claim of completeness).
