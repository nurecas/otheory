steelman: "Predictive processing unifies a wide range of findings (perceptual illusions, attention effects, sensory prediction-error signals, psychiatric symptom models) under one formal framework in which the brain is an inference engine minimizing variational free energy (an upper bound on surprise), using top-down predictions and bottom-up error signals. It has motivated productive computational psychiatry research and specific, testable models of conditions like autism and schizophrenia, and informs serious theories of consciousness (e.g., some Bayesian/hierarchical models of conscious contents as those predictions assigned high precision)."
strongest_objection: "Critics (including systematic philosophical critiques circulating through 2022–2024) argue the framework risks unfalsifiability: because almost any adaptive biological behavior can be redescribed post hoc as 'minimizing free energy' (the 'dark room problem' — organisms should seek zero-stimulation environments to minimize surprise, which they conspicuously do not, requiring added epicycles like 'expected free energy' and built-in priors for exploration), the principle risks being a redescription rather than a predictive theory. The analogy between thermodynamic free energy and information-theoretic 'free energy' has also been challenged as a category-level metaphor transfer rather than a literal identity, and 'preferences'/'goals' introduced into an ostensibly mechanistic framework have been called a hidden teleological move. As a theory specifically of CONSCIOUSNESS (as opposed to general brain function), predictive processing is even less settled — it offers a promising computational vocabulary, not a validated account of why prediction-error minimization should be accompanied by experience. Hence E2: seriously developed and mathematically formalized, not experimentally confirmed as the answer to consciousness."
falsifiability: "Partially — specific predictive-processing models (e.g., particular precision-weighting predictions in perceptual studies, computational-psychiatry symptom models) are falsifiable and tested; the free-energy principle at its most general level has been criticized as approaching an unfalsifiable, near-tautological framework, which is exactly the 'not physics if nothing could falsify it' concern CLAUDE.md's falsifiability rule flags. Classified E2 (mainstream, mathematically developed) rather than E1, and its extension specifically to explaining consciousness (rather than adaptive behavior generally) is the weaker, more speculative part of the claim. Because the falsifiability of the general principle itself is contested (some formulations are argued to be unfalsifiable in principle, others produce testable sub-predictions), status is set to disputed rather than a clean E2/vetted."
