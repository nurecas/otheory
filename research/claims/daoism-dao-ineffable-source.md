The Dàodéjīng opens by asserting that the Dao that can be named or spoken is not the constant, eternal Dao — positioning the Dao as an ineffable source ("mother of all things") that precedes distinction and language, manifesting the world through spontaneous, patterned self-so-ness (ziran) rather than deliberate design. Represented here as classical Daoism's own claim (E5); any O-Theory analogy between "the Dao" and an "O-Source" ground-of-being is a loose metaphor across very different textual and cosmological contexts, not a demonstrated structural equivalence.
