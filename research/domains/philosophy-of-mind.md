# Philosophy of Mind — Domain Survey

Status: SURVEY phase (breadth-first). All claims below are PROVISIONAL pending the vetter's pass.

## Scope of this survey

This file maps the live positions on the mind–body problem relevant to O Theory's philosophical core
(dual-aspect monism / cosmopsychism, "consciousness is fundamental, localized by brains"). Two claims were
already seeded before this survey and are NOT duplicated here: `hard-problem-of-consciousness` (E4) and
`cosmopsychism` (E4). This survey adds the adjacent positions, the arguments that motivate and oppose them,
and the two classic thought-experiments (knowledge argument, zombie argument) that structure the whole
debate, plus Chalmers's reflexive "meta-problem."

Every claim in this domain is philosophical (type: philosophical) and tiered E4 — "not empirically decidable
even in principle, argued by reason" — per CLAUDE.md. None of these are findings; all are positions/arguments.
No claim here should ever be read across into physics or presented as "science shows."

## The map of positions

**Physicalist family** (mind is physical, or wholly determined by the physical):
- `physicalism-functionalism` — the orthodox default in analytic philosophy of mind; functionalism defines
  mental states by causal role, explaining multiple realizability while remaining physicalist about any
  actual realizer.
- `illusionism-eliminativism` — the most radical physicalist-adjacent move: phenomenal consciousness as
  ordinarily conceived does not exist; the "hard problem" is a cognitive illusion to be dissolved, not solved.

**Dualist family** (mind and matter are distinct):
- `property-substance-dualism` — Cartesian substance dualism (two substances, now a minority view owing to
  the interaction problem) and property dualism (one substance, but phenomenal properties are irreducible —
  still taken seriously per SEP).

**Panpsychist / cosmopsychist family** (mentality is fundamental and either ubiquitous or cosmic):
- `panpsychism-combination-problem` — bottom-up: micro-level entities have proto-experiential properties;
  blocked by the combination problem (how do many micro-subjects become one macro-subject).
- `cosmopsychism` (seeded) — top-down: the cosmos as a whole is the fundamental subject; blocked by the
  mirror-image decombination problem.
- `analytic-idealism-kastrup` — consciousness alone is fundamental (no physical substrate at all, even a
  proto-phenomenal one); individual minds are "dissociated alters" of one universal consciousness. The
  position closest to O Theory's own framing; represented as a serious, actively-published minority view with
  its objections given full weight.

**Neutral / dual-aspect family** (a single ground, characterized as neither, or as both):
- `russellian-neutral-monism` — a single neutral (non-mental, non-physical) ground underlies both; Russell's
  own view and its "protophenomenal" descendants (Chalmers, Strawson).
- `dual-aspect-monism` — a single ground has two intrinsic, irreducible aspects, mental and physical, neither
  reducible to the other; traced to Spinoza; modern versions via the Pauli–Jung conjecture (Atmanspacher).
  This is the label O Theory itself uses, so it is filed with the most explicit contrast against its nearest
  neighbor (neutral monism) and its strongest objection (risk of being a redescription, not an explanation,
  of psychophysical correlation).

**The two classic arguments against physicalism** (motivating everything above the physicalist line):
- `knowledge-argument-marys-room` (Jackson) — a complete physical description may not exhaust all facts about
  experience; heavily contested (ability hypothesis, a posteriori physicalism; Jackson himself recanted).
- `zombie-conceivability-argument` (Chalmers) — physical duplicates lacking experience are conceivable, hence
  (arguably) possible, hence physicalism is false; contested by type-B physicalists and Dennett.

**The reflexive move**:
- `meta-problem-of-consciousness` (Chalmers, 2018) — why do we judge/report there is a hard problem at all;
  an "easy" (functionally tractable) problem that nonetheless bears on whether the hard problem is real or
  illusory. Modeled here as the template for separating an observation (people report puzzlement — tractable)
  from its interpretation (what that shows about consciousness — E4).

## Where O Theory sits

O Theory's stated core — dual-aspect monism / cosmopsychism, consciousness fundamental and localized (not
produced) by brains — sits at the intersection of the cosmopsychism, analytic-idealism, and dual-aspect-monism
nodes above. It is best read as a philosophical commitment drawing on a real, live, minority tradition in
analytic metaphysics of mind (Goff's cosmopsychism, Kastrup's analytic idealism, the Pauli–Jung dual-aspect
lineage), not as a position with majority support (physicalism/functionalism remains the default) and not as
an empirically established claim of any kind. Its central vulnerability is structural and shared with its
whole family: the combination/decombination problem, in whichever direction (micro-to-macro or cosmic-to-
individual) the localization move runs. O Theory's "localized by brains" language is itself a decombination-
type claim and inherits that unresolved objection rather than escaping it.

## Key tensions / objections flagged across this domain

1. **The combination/decombination symmetry.** Panpsychism's combination problem and cosmopsychism's
   decombination problem are structurally the same problem run in opposite directions; analytic idealism
   (Kastrup) claims to dissolve both via a dissociation model, but critics argue the DID analogy imports the
   very brain-based individuation it needs to explain, relocating rather than resolving the problem. Any
   O Theory synthesis claiming its "localization" story solves this must show the actual mechanism, not just
   assert the analogy.
2. **Illusionism is the sharpest total rival**, not merely a variant: if illusionism is correct there is no
   phenomenal fact for any of the other positions (including O Theory) to be about. It must never be
   underweighted relative to the "consciousness is fundamental" family it opposes.
3. **Physicalism/functionalism is the field's actual majority position.** Any synthesis document must not
   imply that "consciousness is fundamental" is the mainstream or default view in academic philosophy of
   mind — it is a minority position, seriously defended, but a minority position.
4. **Dual-aspect monism vs. neutral monism is a real, easily blurred distinction**: neutral monism denies the
   ground has two aspects at all (it is neutral, tertium quid); dual-aspect monism affirms two aspects. O
   Theory should be precise about which it means, since the two face different objections.
5. **Jackson recanted his own knowledge argument** (1995) after originally using it to argue for property
   dualism/epiphenomenalism — a useful caution against treating any single thought experiment as settled
   ammunition for non-physicalism.
