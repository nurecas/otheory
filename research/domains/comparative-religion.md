# Comparative Religion — Domain Overview (SURVEY phase)

## Scope of this survey

This survey inventories the family of non-dual / "ground-of-being" traditions relevant to O Theory's
"O-Source" framing, plus the meta-question of whether they can be honestly read as saying "the same
thing." It complements the previously seeded `advaita-nonduality` claim (Advaita Vedānta, E5) with ten
further claims spanning Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna-adjacent Buddhism, Neoplatonism, Kashmir Śaivism, Daoism,
Kabbalah, Christian apophatic mysticism, and Sufism, plus the perennial-philosophy thesis itself filed as
a contested E4 comparative claim.

All claims in this domain are **PROVISIONAL** pending the vetter's pass. Every claim is filed as *that
tradition's own claim* — a symbolic/contemplative report or a piece of traditional metaphysics — never as
an empirical or established fact. Per CLAUDE.md, "Brahman/Dharmakāya/the One/Ein Sof/Dao → O-Source" is
flagged throughout as metaphor or analogy, never as shared mathematics or evidence.

## Genuine convergences (observed, not asserted as proof of identity)

Several structural family resemblances recur across these independently-developed traditions and are
worth naming honestly as *comparative-religion observations*, distinct from any claim that they refer to
one identical reality:

- **A distinction between an ultimate, attribute-less/ineffable ground and a more differentiated,
  nameable manifestation.** Ein Sof vs. the sefirot (Kabbalah); the Godhead vs. God (Eckhart); the Dao
  that cannot be named vs. "the mother of the ten thousand things" (Daoism); Dharmakāya vs. Saṃbhogakāya/
  Nirmāṇakāya (Trikāya); the One vs. Nous/Soul (Plotinus); Brahman nirguṇa vs. saguṇa (Advaita, seed
  claim). This "ground / manifestation" structure recurs, but the traditions disagree sharply on its
  metaphysical status (identity, emanation, illusion, or self-disclosure — see below).
- **Emanationist or dependent-arising cosmogonies rather than creation ex nihilo from a wholly separate
  deity.** Neoplatonic emanation, the sefirotic tree, Śakti's expansion from Śiva, and Sufi tajallī
  (self-disclosure) all describe the world as flowing from or manifesting the ultimate rather than being
  built by an external craftsman — though Madhyamaka's dependent origination is a notably different,
  non-substantialist case that resists even this framing (see Tensions, below).
- **Apophatic/negative method.** Pseudo-Dionysius, Eckhart, the Dàodéjīng's opening lines, Kabbalah's
  Ein Sof, and (in a very different idiom) Madhyamaka's refusal to reify śūnyatā all warn that the
  ultimate cannot be captured in ordinary predication — though they draw very different conclusions from
  that warning (negation toward union vs. negation to prevent any reification at all).
- **A practice/soteriology attached to the metaphysics.** None of these are purely speculative
  metaphysics; each pairs its claim with a contemplative practice (dhyāna, gelassenheit, dhikr, yogic
  recognition/pratyabhijñā, wu-wei) understood as the actual route to verifying or realizing the claim
  first-personally — which is precisely why these remain E5 (contemplative report) rather than E1-E3
  empirical claims.

## Real differences (guard against flattening)

The convergences above should not be read as "they are all secretly saying one thing." Serious,
irreducible disagreements exist:

- **Presence or absence of a personal, willing God.** Kabbalah, Sufism, and Christian apophaticism retain
  (in tension with their own negative theology) a personal, scripturally revealed God; Madhyamaka
  Buddhism and much of Daoism do not posit a creator-deity at all; Kashmir Śaivism's Śiva is personal in
  some respects but framed as universal consciousness rather than a covenantal deity.
- **Is the "ground" a substance/self, or the absence of one?** Advaita's Brahman and Kashmir Śaivism's
  Śiva-consciousness are affirmed as the one real Self; Madhyamaka explicitly denies that śūnyatā is any
  kind of self or substance-ground, and Nāgārjuna's Middle Way is partly an argument *against* reifying
  any ultimate principle, including emptiness itself. This is a direct, first-order disagreement, not a
  surface difference of vocabulary — treating Śūnyatā and Brahman as equivalent is a common but
  contestable move that scholars of Buddhism specifically warn against.
- **Is multiplicity real, illusory, or "self-disclosure"?** Advaita's māyā (superimposition/illusion),
  Kashmir Śaivism's affirmation of Śakti's manifestation as real creative play (spanda), Sufism's fixed
  entities as modes of divine self-disclosure, and Yogācāra's "transformations of consciousness" are four
  distinct ontological accounts of why/how a many appears from or within a one — not interchangeable
  restatements.
- **Historical contestation within each tradition.** Several of these claims were controversial even on
  their home turf: Eckhart faced posthumous condemnation of some propositions by Pope John XXII (1329);
  waḥdat al-wujūd was fiercely attacked by Ibn Taymiyya and other Islamic scholars, and Ibn Arabi himself
  never used the phrase (it was coined by later commentators/critics); Dvaita Vedānta (Madhva) is a major
  rival within Hindu philosophy that rejects Advaita's identity thesis outright. "Non-dual traditions"
  is not a monolith even within a single religion.

## The perennial philosophy thesis: status

The `perennial-philosophy-common-core-thesis` claim is filed at **E4** (philosophical/comparative,
not E5), because — unlike the traditions' own first-order claims — it is a second-order comparative
thesis *about* those traditions, argued by scholars/philosophers of religion (Huxley 1945, Schuon 1975,
Stace) rather than a report internal to any one contemplative practice. The dominant scholarly rebuttal is
Steven Katz's **constructivism** (1978 and after): mystical experience is mediated by the mystic's prior
conceptual scheme "all the way down," so apparent cross-traditional agreement may be an artifact of
comparison rather than evidence of one shared reality. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's
"Mysticism" entry frames this as an open, unresolved methodological impasse: we only ever have mystics'
*post-experiential, already-interpreted* reports, never raw pre-linguistic experience to compare
directly — so the debate is not merely undecided but may be undecidable with current methods. O Theory's
practice of mapping Brahman/Dharmakāya/the One/Ein Sof/Dao onto a single "O-Source" is, transparently, an
adoption of the perennialist side of this live debate; it should be labeled a **metaphor/analogy** (per
CLAUDE.md's register rules), never as a settled comparative-religion consensus.

  Arabi) and confirm copyright status before any direct quotation.

  case in the brief but not yet filed as its own claim — a natural deep-dive addition to sharpen the
  "not all Hindu philosophy is non-dual" point already made in Tensions above.
- **Vajrayāna/Dzogchen and Zen (esp. Hua-yen or Chan articulations of Buddha-nature/tathāgatagarbha)**
  were not covered in this pass and would strengthen Buddhist coverage alongside Madhyamaka, Trikāya, and
  Yogācāra.
- **Trikāya's secondary sourcing (Britannica)** should be upgraded to a peer-reviewed academic source
  (e.g. a Buddhist-studies monograph or a Routledge/Oxford handbook chapter) in deep-dive.
- **Whitehead/process theology and other Western panentheisms** were out of scope for this pass but are
  adjacent to the ground-of-being family and worth a future claim if O Theory's synthesis wants a modern
  Western philosophical bridge distinct from Neoplatonism.
- The **Zohar translation used (de Manhar, sacred-texts.com)** is explicitly flagged by its own hosting
  site as a non-critical, pseudonymous Theosophist rendering with anachronistic terminology; deep-dive
  should prefer the Pritzker or Soncino scholarly editions (noting copyright constraints on the Soncino
  edition) for any direct quotation.

## Domain-level tensions for the synthesizer to note

1. The **"ground vs. manifestation" structural resemblance is real but the ontological cash-value
   differs radically** (identity in Advaita/Kashmir Śaivism vs. explicit anti-reification in Madhyamaka
   vs. creation/self-disclosure in the Abrahamic-mystical cases) — a synthesis must not average these into
   one position.
2. The **perennialism vs. constructivism debate is unresolved in religious-studies scholarship itself**
   (per SEP), which means O Theory's perennialist framing is an inherited, contestable interpretive
   choice, not a neutral summary of expert consensus — this should be stated explicitly wherever the
   synthesis leans on "these traditions converge."
3. **Historical orthodoxy disputes within each tradition** (Eckhart's condemned propositions, the
   anti-waḥdat-al-wujūd polemics, Dvaita's rejection of Advaita) show that even calling a tradition's
   non-dual reading "the" reading of that tradition can overstate internal consensus.
