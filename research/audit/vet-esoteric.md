Both are corrected below.

### Source verification highlights

- **Astrology (Carlson 1985, Nature):** confirmed as a real, landmark double-blind study — 28 professional astrologers nominated by their own professional body (National Council for Geocosmic Research), matching natal charts to CPI personality profiles under blind conditions, pre-registered success criterion, chance-level result. The Ertel (2009) reanalysis dispute is real and correctly flagged as contested rather than a reversal. E6 confirmed.
- **Sheldrake / morphic resonance — citation error found and fixed:** the draft attributed the key 2021 retrospective to "Freeman, A. (2021)." The actual author, confirmed via PubMed and the publisher (Wiley/BioEssays), is **Alex Gomez-Marin**. The PMID/DOI pointed to the correct paper, so this was a misattributed-author error rather than a fabricated source, but it had to be corrected before the file could be marked verified. Separately, the specific statistic "~45 of 50 replications failed" could not be traced to any citable primary meta-analysis (it circulates in secondary/blog sources but not in the cited Wikipedia article or any peer-reviewed source I could locate) — removed as an unsupported precise figure and replaced with the well-documented qualitative claim plus the two concrete, checkable failed replications (Wiseman's staring-detection study; the Wiseman/Smith/Milton Jaytee dog-telepathy replication).
- **Chakra measurability — source mischaracterization found and fixed:** both chakra files cited Moga (2022), "Is there scientific evidence for chakras?" (International Journal of Healing and Caring), as if it were a neutral or skeptical review of "attempted" correlates. Having read the full PDF, this is wrong on two counts: (1) IJHC is the house journal of the National Alliance of Energy Healing/Energy Practitioners, an energy-healing practitioner-advocacy organization, not an independent scientific journal; (2) the paper's own conclusion argues affirmatively that chakras "may be scientifically delineated" and that "radiations are detectable at chakra sites" — it is making the positive (E6) case, not surveying it skeptically, and its own cited evidence includes a self-published book (Hunt 1996) and a non-indexed engineering-society journal (Jalil et al. 2015). Removed this source from the E5 contemplative-map claim entirely (a practice/symbol claim doesn't need a contested measurement paper) and re-cited it accurately in the E6 claim as the weak, non-independent, advocacy-affiliated source making the positive claim — which, read honestly, strengthens rather than weakens the E6 verdict, since it is the strongest available "positive" evidence and does not hold up. Medical News Today (mainstream-view source, "no scientific evidence for chakras in the traditional sense") was verified and retained as the accurate corrective.
- **Corpus Hermeticum / Casaubon:** confirmed via Grafton (1983), Journal of the Warburg and Courtauld Institutes — added as a stronger, directly-checkable peer-reviewed citation for the 1614 dating claim, replacing a bare assertion. Added a needed qualification: Casaubon's forgery charge, per later scholar Ralph Cudworth, cleanly applies to only 3 of the 17 treatises, so "the whole corpus is a late forgery" overstates the actual finding — corrected to "later than claimed, not a single forged unit."
- **Kabbalah / Scholem:** the draft's Scholem source was a PDF on a non-scholarly site (betemunah.org) that returned as corrupted/unreadable on direct fetch. Replaced with a direct citation to Scholem's actual book, Major Trends in Jewish Mysticism (1941), independently confirmed via search as the foundational source for the Moses de Leon/Zohar dating (c. 1280-1286), which is the mainstream scholarly consensus.
- **Alchemy / Newman & Principe:** the draft's two book sources were marked `verified: false`; independently confirmed both books are genuine and correctly attributed, and located the actual peer-reviewed foundational article (Newman & Principe, "Alchemy vs. Chemistry," Early Science and Medicine 3(1), 1998) to add a directly-checkable peer-reviewed citation alongside the books.
- **Kybalion / Atkinson, Theosophy / Blavatsky 1875, Hermetic correspondence / Emerald Tablet, sacred-geometry cosmic-blueprint / Tokenrock, Markowsky 1992, gematria / Wikipedia, ley lines / Watkins 1921:** all checked directly against source content and confirmed accurate as cited. Minor upgrades: My Jewish Learning (gematria) and the Skeptic's Dictionary / Science Feedback (ley lines) upgraded from `verified: false` to `verified: true` since the specific narrow facts they support are correct and independently corroborated, even though they are popularizer-tier sources not scholarly ones — acceptable per CLAUDE.md because none of them carries a contested claim alone.

### The Sheldrake E3-vs-E6 decision (explicit re-examination, as instructed)

Kept at **E6**, not moved to E3-with-heavy-caveats. Reasoning: the case for E3 would rest on (1) a credentialed proponent (Cambridge PhD biologist), (2) a genuine open question in developmental biology (how form, not just molecular sequence, is specified), and (3) a 2021 peer-reviewed retrospective (Gomez-Marin, BioEssays) arguing the hypothesis has faced "dogmatic" dismissal rather than sustained testing. Weighed against this: the specific, named flagship experiments Sheldrake himself proposed as tests — staring detection and the Jaytee dog-telepathy claim — HAVE been independently run and DID fail (Wiseman et al.), so this is not an untested hypothesis in the areas where it made concrete, checkable predictions. More decisively, the general "morphic field" mechanism specifies no physical carrier, no equations, and no detection method even in principle, unlike electromagnetic or gravitational fields — it fails the falsifiability rule not for lack of trying but because nothing testable is stated for the general mechanism. Mainstream biology and physics reject it as pseudoscience (Maddox 1981, Nature editorial), and no alternative physically-consistent operationalization has been offered by anyone in 40+ years. E3 is reserved in this project for contested-but-mechanistically-stated minority positions (e.g., Orch-OR proposes a specific, disputed physical mechanism); Sheldrake's hypothesis does not clear that bar. The genuine open developmental-biology question is real and preserved in the steelman, but it is a separate claim from the specific mechanism, which fails on both replication and falsifiability grounds. **E6, status rejected.**
